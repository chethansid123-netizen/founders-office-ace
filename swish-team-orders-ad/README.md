# Swish Team Orders: launch ad, 15 s and 30 s cuts (spec concept)

**Deliverables:** two vertical cuts, both 1080×1920, 60 fps, H.264 + AAC stereo. They're built for Instagram Reels, LinkedIn mobile, WhatsApp Status and office lift screens.
- **30-second cut:** `swish_team_orders_30s_9x16.mp4` (16 bars). This is the full story, for LinkedIn, lift screens and anything that plays with sound. See section 5b.
- **15-second cut:** `swish_team_orders_15s_9x16.mp4` (8 bars). This is the short version for Reels and Stories.

**Companion to:** *Should Swish introduce TEAM Orders?* (market research deck: 169 survey responses, 80 interviews, 25 depth interviews).
**Status:** Spec ad concept for the founders. Team Orders is a proposed feature, and the end card says so.

## 1. The idea in one line

> **"You didn't become a team lead to take lunch orders."**

The ad talks to the **host**, the one person in every team who ends up collecting orders, fronting the money and chasing everyone for it. It shows that pain in two seconds, then shows Team Orders fixing it in three taps. It ends on the deck's own line: **One link. One delivery. One bill.**

## 2. What the research says about Swish's brand

| Brand element | What Swish does today | What the ad does with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Promise** | "10-minute food delivery". Swish runs its own kitchens, riders and app with no third parties, from compact kitchens that each cover about 1 km. | The rider arrives in *9 min*, and the end line is *"Lunch for the whole team. In 10 minutes."* |
| **Colour** | A saturated fresh green that stands apart from the red and orange of Zomato and Swiggy. | Colour carries the story: dark and grey for the chaos, **Swish green** for the fix. |
| **Logo** | A white lowercase rounded wordmark with a four-point **sparkle**, which design write-ups read as the "delight" of food arriving before you expect it. | The logo was traced to a clean vector from the app icon. The **sparkle becomes the transition device**: it spins in, then grows to wipe the screen at both scene changes. |
| **Copy system** | "Fresh. Quick. Delicious." · "Fresh food in minutes" · "one for ~You, one for ~Them" on the referral hang-tag. | The copy stays short and rhythmic: *One link. One delivery. One bill.* |
| **Voice** | Playful, direct and offer-led (*"Refer a friend, get ₹250 and free cold coffee"*). It is local too: Instagram presents Swish as "neighbourhood kitchens". | The chat uses real Bengaluru office lines: *"@you order kar diya?"*, *"Jain option hai?"*, *"I'll pay you later 😅"* |
| **Channels** | Street-level out-of-home (the glowing "human billboards" walking Bengaluru at night went viral, and also drew criticism), branded bags with referral hang-tags, in-app banners (referral, Instagram follow), Instagram, and the shared order-status screen. | See the distribution plan in section 6. The same surfaces can carry Team Orders. |

**A brand risk the ad works around.** Interviewees in the deck kept confusing Swish with Bistro ("both are green, same colour"). Green alone is not enough to be recognised, so the ad repeats three assets a rival cannot copy: the **sparkle**, the **wordmark**, and a **sonic logo** (a whoosh followed by a four-note sparkle chime, played at the reveal and again on the end card).

**Business context** (public reporting, 2026, via Inc42, TechCrunch and YourStory): Swish takes about 20,000 orders a day, up from about 5,000 four months earlier. It operates in about 50 pincodes across Bengaluru and Delhi NCR. It raised a $38M Series B (Hara Global, Bain Capital Ventures, Accel) and then another round, while several rival quick-food services have shut or scaled back. The deck's point stands: speed alone no longer sets anyone apart. **Serving the group from one kitchen** is something aggregators find hard to copy.

## 3. Who the ad is for, from the deck's findings

- **The target is a role, not a demographic.** Demographics did not predict adoption (H4). What matters is who orders for the group, and **60%** say one person orders for everyone.
- **The pain is coordination and money, not finding food.** Money-collection annoyance averaged **3.65/5**. Team leads front about **₹4,000** and wait **10–30 days** for the expense claim.
- **The strongest pulls are practical.** **80%** would choose a group order if a bulk discount applied, **72%** want the bill sent straight to the company, and **79%** rate the feature set as important.
- **Scope is 2–10 people.** 67% put the group-size ceiling at ten or fewer, so the ad shows 8.

## 4. The persuasion techniques, and where each one appears

| # | Technique | Where | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Identity call-out hook** | 0.0–1.9 s | "You didn't become a team lead…" picks out the right viewer in the first second, and the humour earns the next second. |
| 2 | **Show the chaos in their own words** | 0.0–1.9 s | The 12 chat bubbles come straight from interview verbatims. The pops speed up, so the anxiety builds. |
| 3 | **Specific numbers** | 1.9–3.75 s | *8 people. 8 riders. ₹4,000 on your card.* Concrete figures land harder than "ordering together is hard". |
| 4 | **A brand-owned transition** | 3.4–3.75 s | The sparkle wipes away the chaos and becomes the reveal, so the relief is tied to the logo. |
| 5 | **Three-step demo** | 5.6–11.25 s | Share one link → everyone adds their own → one rider, one bill. It shows ease instead of claiming it. |
| 6 | **Proof points in the UI** | Demo | ₹300 cap, 30-minute link, veg-mark icons, "Added by…" names, GST invoice, "Billed to Your Company". Each one is a finding from the deck. |
| 7 | **Callback payoff** | 10.5 s and 12.7 s | *"₹4,000 on your card"* returns as a stamp and a slam: **"₹0 on your card."** |
| 8 | **Rule of three plus a twist** | 11.25–13.1 s | *One link. One delivery. One bill.* ... *And the best part?* **₹0 on your card.** |
| 9 | **Sonic branding** | 3.75 s and 13.1 s | The same whoosh and chime at the reveal and the logo builds recall even with the sound off. |
| 10 | **Readable with the sound off** | Throughout | Every beat is readable muted, which matters because most feed video autoplays silently. |
| 11 | **Platform safe zones** | Throughout | Key text sits between y = 236 and y = 1500, clear of the Reels and LinkedIn interface. |
| 12 | **Loops cleanly** | 15.0 s → 0.0 s | The calm end card cuts straight back to the chaos, so a second watch repeats the contrast. |
| 13 | **Honest claims** | Throughout | There are no invented statistics. The discount is an absolute amount (**−₹112** = 8 × ₹14, from the deck's modelled 60/40 split), with no struck-through "was" price, in line with the deck's dark-pattern compliance point. The end card says *spec ad concept*. |

## 5b. The 30-second cut: what the extra 15 seconds add

The 30-second cut is not the 15-second cut slowed down. It is 16 bars on the same 128 BPM grid, so every beat still lands on the music. The extra bars do two jobs: they give each message time to be read, and they add the proof a longer ad can afford.

| Time | Bars | Scene | What's new compared with the 15 s cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0–3.75 | 1–2 | **Hook** | The chat has 16 bubbles, ending in *"ok I'll just order myself"*. The headline stays on screen for about 2 seconds. |
| 3.75–7.5 | 3–4 | **Pain** | The cards land every other beat, so each one can be read. A new line, **"Sound familiar?"**, lets the viewer recognise the situation. |
| 7.5–9.4 | 5 | **Reveal** | The sparkle wipe opens on **Team Orders** and the team gathers into a row. |
| 9.4–13.1 | 6–7 | **① Share one link** | **A live spend-cap slider** (₹150 → ₹300) and a "Company pays" chip. The link lands in the chat, gets *Seen by 8*, and picks up 👍 🙌 🔥 reactions. |
| 13.1–16.9 | 8–9 | **② Everyone adds their own** | The 8 items arrive one per 8th note. Then the phone slides aside for three **feature cards**: *Veg packed separately*, *A name on every box*, *Cart locks before dispatch*. The host taps and the order is placed. |
| 16.9–20.6 | 10–11 | **③ One rider. One bill.** | The ETA counts down from 9 min to **Delivered ✅ · on time**, reflecting the deck's finding that arriving on time matters more than raw speed. Then the **₹0 on your card** stamp. |
| 20.6–22.5 | 12 | **Why only Swish?** *(new scene)* | **"Every dish. One kitchen. One rider."** Five dishes drop into a **Swish bag** drawn after the real one ("FRESH QUICK DELICIOUS · 10-MINUTE FOOD DELIVERY"). It hops onto one scooter and rides off. This is the deck's structural advantage: aggregators can't do it, because their food comes from many restaurants. |
| 22.5–26.25 | 13–14 | **Slam** | One link. One delivery. One bill. *And the best part?* **₹0 on your card.** A new line follows: *No chasing. No expense claims.* |
| 26.25–30.0 | 15–16 | **End card** | The sonic logo plays over the CTA, and the team's avatars reappear under the button as a bookend to the reveal. It closes on a warm B♭ → F cadence with a final sparkle chime. |

**Music arc:** tension under the hook and cards → sparkle drop at 7.5 s → a groove that gets a 16th-note arpeggio for the second half of the demo → a stripped-back breakdown for "Why only Swish?" → slams → the sonic logo and closing cadence.

**Why run both cuts:** the 15-second cut is for placements where people skip (Reels, Stories). The 30-second cut is for placements with a captive viewer (LinkedIn feed, lift screens, pitch meetings), where the **reason to believe** ("Why only Swish?") is what turns interest into trust.

## 6. Where office buyers would see it (distribution plan)

The deck's go-to-market is two geofenced tech parks first, so media should cover the host's working day inside those parks.

| Moment | Placement | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Morning commute | **Lift and lobby screens** in the two pilot parks | This ad, muted, on loop |
| Scrolling at the desk | **LinkedIn video ads** targeted by title (Team Lead, Engineering Manager, Office/Admin Manager, HR) and by company inside the geofence | 9:16 on mobile |
| 12:00–12:30 PM | **In-app banner and push** to known multi-item orderers: *"Ordering for the team? Start a Team Order."* | 6-second cut-down: hook + step 1 + end card |
| The lunch chat itself | **The shared link is the ad.** Every Team Order link unfurled in WhatsApp, Slack or Teams puts Swish in front of 2–9 colleagues. | Link preview card in brand green with the sparkle |
| Delivery | **Bag hang-tag with a QR code.** Swish already uses hang-tags for referrals: *"Next time, order for the whole team."* | Print |
| Evenings and weekends | **Instagram Reels and Stories**, geofenced. The deck found work and social group ordering go together (r = .56). | This ad |

**Measure it against the deck's north star.** Track ad click-through → Team Orders started → carts that lock with 2+ orders → **orders per delivery trip** (target ≥ 1.8 by week 6, 3.0+ long term).

## 7. Variants to A/B test in the pilot

- **Hook A (identity)** is the one in this video: *"You didn't become a team lead to take lunch orders."*
- **Hook B (money):** *"Still waiting on last month's lunch reimbursement?"*
- **Hook C (logistics):** *"8 riders. 1 gate. 1:15 PM."*
- **End-card offer:** none, or *"Team discount on every Team Order"*. The deck warns that leading with discounts can teach people to wait for offers.

**Optional voiceover** (about 38 words, fits 15 s). The ad works without it; record a human voice if you want one.
> "You didn't become a team lead to take lunch orders. Meet Team Orders, from Swish. Share one link. Everyone adds their own. One rider. One bill, straight to your company. Lunch for the whole team, in ten minutes."

## 9. How to edit and re-render

The ad is an HTML page whose `render(t)` function draws any moment of the 15 seconds. `render.js` captures each frame in headless Chromium and pipes the frames to ffmpeg with the soundtrack.

```bash
cd source
export FFMPEG=$(python3 -c "import imageio_ffmpeg;print(imageio_ffmpeg.get_ffmpeg_exe())")   # needs playwright + chromium

# 15-second cut
python3 build.py                      # injects the vector logo into ad.template.html → ad.html
python3 audio.py                      # → soundtrack.wav (every hit sits on the same timeline as the visuals)
node render.js ../swish_team_orders_15s_9x16.mp4 60

# 30-second cut (shares the CSS of ad.template.html; its scenes and script live in ad30.body.html)
python3 build30.py                    # → ad30.template.html → ad30.html
python3 audio30.py soundtrack30.wav
DUR=30 AUDIO=soundtrack30.wav node render.js ../swish_team_orders_30s_9x16.mp4 60 ad30.html

python3 storyboard.py ../swish_team_orders_30s_9x16.mp4 .. 30   # storyboard_30s.jpg + poster_30s.jpg
```

- **Change the copy:** edit the text in `ad.template.html` (15 s) or `ad30.body.html` (30 s). The hook is `#hook`, the pain cards are `#c1`–`#c3`, the captions are `#cap1`–`#cap3`, the slams are `#s7`, the end card is `#s8`. The 30 s "Why only Swish?" scene is `#s9`.
- **Preview in a browser:** open `ad.html?play` (or `ad30.html?play`) to loop it live, or `ad.html?t=12.8` to jump to one moment.
- **Change timing:** all times are in seconds on a 128 BPM grid (1 beat = 0.46875 s). The 30 s cut keeps them in one `TM` table at the top of its script. If you move a visual beat, move the matching hit in `audio.py` / `audio30.py`.
