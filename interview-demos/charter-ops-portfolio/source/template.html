Ops kit: The F&B Sit-Down, Bengaluru

One gathering from your calendar, planned end to end: a chef turns the products of seven D2C brands into a seven-course dinner, and founders and investors sit across from each other. I picked it because it needs a venue, a chef, seven brands, pours, gifting and vendors, which is the sponsor and venue work the role asks for.

Prepared on 4 November 2026. No venue, partner, chef or guest has been contacted. Brands marked "public list" appear on The Charter's partnerships page and are listed here only as candidates. Statuses are simulated.

The evening

  • Seven courses, one per brand, about twenty minutes each.
  • Guests don't know which founder is behind which course until a reveal card at the end, the moment a member described on your site.
  • One seat switch after course three, so each investor talks to two founders properly.
  • Two-minute welcome from the host. No other speeches.

What I'd hold myself to

  • 22 or more of 24 seats filled on the night, waitlist included.
  • Everyone seated by 8:15 pm.
  • All seven brand products at the venue by T-3.
  • Intros asked for at the table actioned within 72 hours.
  • Every partner recapped within five days.
  • Cost per seat within the agreed budget.

Who covers what

  • Venue: food and beverage minimum spend, paid.
  • Chef: fee, paid.
  • Course products and exit gifts: in kind, from the brands.
  • Pours: in kind from the pour partner; corkage agreed with the venue.
  • Print, florals, photography: paid, three quotes each.
  • Valet: venue, or two attendants.

Amounts go in once the quotes are back.

On the night

  • Host: Bengaluru city lead

If something slips

  • A guest drops at T-1: the first of four waitlisted guests gets a call by 6 pm.
  • A brand's product is short: the chef's backup course goes in.
  • A VIP is late: their seat is held until course two.
  • Friday traffic: the drop-off pin went out with the reminder.

Consent, in practice

  • The photographer shoots the room and the food. Faces only for guests who ticked yes at RSVP.
  • Partners get a recap by role, never a guest list.
  • Nothing goes on social during the evening.

How I'd choose the venue

  • 24 seated at one long table, with the room to ourselves from 6 pm to close.
  • A kitchen that will host a guest chef and take delivery of brand products at T-3.
  • Quiet enough to talk across a table, and valet or a clean drop-off.
  • A licensed bar that will pour partner product, with corkage agreed in writing.
  • Scored on all four at a site visit, with photos, then a hold on the date at two venues until one signs.

What every partner brief says

  • The ask, the delivery slot and the contact on the day.
  • What they get: their moment in the evening, a credit on the menu card, a recap within five days.
  • What they don't get: banners, pitching, the guest list.
  • Your partnerships page promises a reply within two days. The tracker holds us to it.

From brief to close-out

Dates follow the mock date above. Change it and the plan moves.

    The table

    • Founders sit opposite investors, as the format describes.
    • Each brand founder sits opposite an investor who hasn't been told which course is theirs.
    • No two competitors side by side; first-timers next to someone who has been to three or more evenings.
    • Seating is drafted with the city lead from the member list, the way your Inside page describes it.

    Dietary sheet

    • Asked in the invitation, confirmed at T-10.
    • One row per seat: vegetarian, Jain, vegan, allergies, no alcohol.
    • The chef plans each course's swap before the tasting at T-14.
    • The sheet goes to the pass and to the floor captain on the night.

    Rules that shape the logistics

    • Article I, confidence. Photo consent, partner recaps without names, nothing posted from the room.
    • Article VII, presence. Guests come prepared and on time, so reminders, reconfirmation and a waitlist matter.
    • No pitching. Partner briefs rule out banners and sales.
    • No fees for introductions. Follow-up intros are a service to members, routed through the host.
    • Hand-built rooms. "We sit with the member list" before every evening, so guest lists and seating need time in the plan.

    What I think this means for the role

    1. Repeatable beats heroic

      Four gatherings a month across three cities need a standard kit: a run-of-show template, a scored venue bench and one partner tracker. That's how Techvaraha went from 65% to 90% on time.

    2. Partners are the busiest workstream

      Fifteen partners on one Diwali night is fifteen briefs, fifteen delivery slots and fifteen recaps. A partner who isn't recapped rarely comes back.

    3. Bengaluru's bench is still being built

      The city opened in September 2025. A scored list of venues, chefs and vendors there is the quickest win, and I live there.

    4. Room size swings

      From a Table of seven to a 300-guest night, and a three-day retreat with weather risk. The kit needs a small version and a large version.

    5. Curation needs lead time

      Rooms are built by hand from the member list. Ops has to protect that time with early invitations and an RSVP close, not squeeze it.

    6. The playbook will travel

      International expansion was announced in April. SOPs written for Bengaluru should work in a city nobody on the team has run yet.
