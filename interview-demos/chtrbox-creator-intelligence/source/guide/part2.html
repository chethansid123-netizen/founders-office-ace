Part 2

Every metric, term and formula

This is the reference you study. It defines every number the platform shows, gives the exact formula the engine uses, works each one through with real values from the data files, and gives you the sentence to say when someone asks about it.

How to read this part

Each metric has a definition box (plain meaning, formula, example), then three short lines: why it matters, what good looks like, and how to say it. The "good" benchmarks come from two places. The tier medians in data/core.json (creatorStats), and medians computed across the 420 creators in data.json. Every number on the platform is simulated. The creators are synthetic personas, and the brand names are real Chtrbox clients or labelled prospects with modelled metrics. When you quote a number in the room, say "in the simulation" or "in this prototype" once at the start, and repeat it whenever someone looks like they are taking a number as a real result.

2.1 Creator metrics

A creator is one person who posts content (a Reel, a Story, a YouTube video) to an audience. Everything Chtrbox sells rests on choosing the right creators. The engine describes each creator with about twenty numbers. Here they are in the order you will meet them on the creator profile (the drawer that opens when you click a name).

Followers and tiers

Followers and tier

Followers is the number of accounts that follow the creator on their main platform. Tier is a size band based on followers. The industry uses tiers because creators of different sizes behave differently: small creators get more engagement per follower and charge less, large ones reach more people per post. Formula: tier = the band the follower count falls in (Nano 3K–10K, Micro 10K–100K, Mid-tier 100K–500K, Macro 500K–1M, Mega 1M+). Example: Kunal K. (CIE-1332) has 32,206 Instagram followers, so he is Micro.

"Tier tells us the price band and what to expect. It doesn't tell us who is good. That's what the other numbers are for."

Engagement rate (ER)

Engagement rate

The share of a creator's followers who interact with a typical post (like, comment, save, share). It is the most quoted creator metric in India. Formula: ER = engagements per post ÷ followers × 100. Example: Kunal K. has ER 3.45%, so a post gets about 32,206 × 3.45% ≈ 1,111 engagements.

Why it matters. ER shows whether the audience is paying attention. In the engine it counts toward Performance for consideration (45% of that part) and conversion (20%). Good. Above the tier median. Kunal's 3.45% beats the Micro median of 2.82%. The generator's tier medians are 4.9%, 2.8%, 1.4%, 0.9% and 0.4%. Say it. "A 1% ER is weak for a micro creator and strong for a mega creator, so we only ever compare ER inside a tier."

Average views

Average views

How many times a typical Reel (or video) is watched. Formula: mean views over recent posts. Example: Kunal K. averages 7,038 views per Reel, about 22% of his followers.

Why it matters. Views are the raw reach the forecast starts from. Good. Median views per follower in the sample are 0.33 (Nano), 0.25 (Micro), 0.19 (Mid-tier), 0.18 (Macro) and 0.12 (Mega). Median Reel views are 1,922, 6,152, 46,852, 1,13,548 and 4,95,069 by tier. Say it. "We start from what a creator's Reels actually get, not from follower count."

View-through rate (VTR)

View-through rate

The share of each video that the average viewer watches. 44% means people watch 44% of the video's length on average. Formula: VTR = average watch time ÷ video length × 100. Example: Kunal K. has VTR 27.7%.

Why it matters. A brand message that sits in the middle of a video is only seen if people stay. VTR is 35% of the Performance part for awareness. Good. The sample median is 44.2% (range 22–72%). Kunal at 27.7% is weak here, which is why he ranks high for conversion and not for awareness. Say it. "View-through is how much of the video people actually watch. For awareness, that's where the brand is seen."

3-second hold (hook strength)

Hold at 3 seconds

The share of viewers still watching three seconds in. It measures how strong the opening is. Formula: viewers at 3 s ÷ viewers who started × 100. Example: Kunal K. holds 40.9%.

Why it matters. Most scrolling decisions happen in the first three seconds. It is 25% of awareness Performance. Good. Sample median 63.5% (range 38–88%). Say it. "The hook number tells us if people stop scrolling. If they don't, nothing after second three matters."

Saves and shares per view

Saves and shares per view

The share of viewers who save a post (to come back to it) or send it to someone. Formula: saves ÷ views × 100, shares ÷ views × 100. The engine adds the two. Example: Kunal K. saves 2.27% + shares 0.64% = 2.91%.

Why it matters. A save is a strong signal of intent ("I want to buy this later"). A share spreads the post for free. Together they are 35% of consideration Performance. Good. Sample medians are 1.42% saves and 0.75% shares. Finance and education creators save most (category base rates 2.4% and 2.2%). Say it. "A like is cheap. A save means someone wants to come back. That's why saves weigh more for consideration."

Link CTR

Link click-through rate

The share of viewers who tap the link (a Story link sticker, a bio link or a YouTube description link). Formula: CTR = link clicks ÷ views × 100. Example: Kunal K. has CTR 1.58%, top 1% of Micro creators.

Why it matters. Clicks are the first step toward an order. CTR is 55% of conversion Performance, the single largest input to a conversion ranking. Good. Sample median 0.78% (range 0.12–2.6%). Above 1.2% is strong in any tier. Say it. "For a sale, the one behaviour that matters most is whether people tap the link. That's CTR."

Comment sentiment

Comment sentiment

The share of comments that are positive, scored by a language model that reads Hinglish and regional languages. Formula: positive comments ÷ all scored comments × 100. Example: Kunal K. scores 90.3.

Why it matters. It shows whether the audience likes and trusts the creator. It is 20% of consideration Performance and also feeds the trust index. Good. Sample median 80 (range 58–95). Say it. "We read the comments, not just count them. Positive sentiment means the audience is on the creator's side."

Content quality

Content quality score

A 0–100 score for production quality (framing, lighting, sound, editing), scored by a vision model over frames. Formula: model score, 0–100. Example: Kunal K. scores 60.

Why it matters. Low-effort content is a common sign of a bought account, so in the simulation fraud accounts lose 22 points. It is 20% of the Authenticity part. Good. Sample median 68. Say it. "Quality matters for the brand, and very poor quality is often a fraud signal too."

Real-followers share (authenticity)

Real and active followers

The share of followers who are real people and active, after removing bots, bought followers and dormant accounts. Formula: (followers − bots − inactive) ÷ followers × 100. Example: Kunal K. is at 90.9%, so about 2,900 of his 32,206 followers are not real or active.

Why it matters. Every fake follower is money spent on nobody. It is the hard gate at 72% and 60% of the Authenticity part. Good. Tier means run from 86.4% (Nano) to 75.3% (Mega). The generator gives larger accounts more fraud: 6% of nano, 7% micro, 10% mid, 14% macro and 16% mega creators are fraud accounts. In the sample, 32 of 420 creators fall below 72%. Say it. "Before we look at anything else, we ask how many of these followers are real. Below 72%, the creator is out."

Sponsored-post saturation

Sponsored-post share

The share of a creator's recent posts that are paid brand posts. Formula: sponsored posts ÷ recent posts × 100. Example: Kunal K. is at 44%.

Why it matters. When almost every post is an ad, followers tune out (ad fatigue) and trust drops. It is 20% of the Authenticity part, scored as 1 − share ÷ 80, so 80% or more scores zero. Above 45% the engine raises an "Ad fatigue" flag. Good. Sample median 28%; 24 creators are above 45%. Kunal's 44% sits just under the flag line, which is why his authenticity part is 74 and not higher. Say it. "If half a creator's feed is ads, the next ad works less. We price that in."

#ad disclosure rate

ASCI disclosure rate

The share of the creator's paid posts that carry a clear disclosure such as #ad or "Paid partnership", as required by ASCI (the Advertising Standards Council of India) guidelines for influencers. Formula: disclosed paid posts ÷ paid posts × 100. Example: Kunal K. discloses on 92%.

Why it matters. Non-disclosure exposes the brand to an ASCI complaint. It is 40% of the Safety part. Below 75% the engine raises a flag telling the team to brief disclosure in the first line. Good. Sample median 87%; 28 creators are below 75%. Say it. "Disclosure is a compliance number for the brand. We track it per creator and brief anyone below 75%."

Brand-safety score

Brand-safety score

A 0–100 score for how safe the creator's recent content is for a brand, from AI review plus human checks. Content flags pull it down. Formula (simulation): base score around 88, minus 12–22 for profanity, 10–18 for political commentary, 18–28 for a past controversy, 14–22 for promoting betting or real-money gaming, 10–18 for unverified health claims. Floor 35, cap 99. Example: Kunal K. scores 88 with no flags.

Why it matters. One bad creator can become a brand's crisis. Below 55 is a hard gate; it is 60% of the Safety part. Good. Sample median 86. Only 4 creators fall under 55. Say it. "Safety is scored from the content itself, and anything under 55 never reaches the shortlist."

On-time delivery

On-time delivery

The share of past deliverables the creator posted on the agreed date. It comes from Chtrbox's own operations data. Formula: posts live on agreed date ÷ posts contracted × 100. Example: Kunal K. is at 92%.

Why it matters. A festive-sale post that goes live after the sale earns nothing. It is shown on the profile and sits in the Commercials family. In this prototype it does not enter the fit score. Good. Sample median 90% (range 68–100%). Say it. "This is our own ops data. Today it's on the profile for the strategist; it's an obvious next input to the score."

Recall lift from past studies

Past recall lift

How many percentage points the creator raised ad recall in past brand-lift studies (people who saw the post minus people who didn't, asked "do you remember seeing an ad for this brand?"). Formula: recall % in exposed group − recall % in control group, averaged over past studies. Example: Kunal K. has +2.4 pts. Sample range 2–17.

Why it matters. It is the best evidence that a creator makes people remember a brand. It is 40% of awareness Performance, the largest share. Good. Sample median +5.5 pts. Say it. "Recall lift is measured, not guessed. It comes from control-versus-exposed surveys on past campaigns."

Trust index

Trust index

A multiplier for how likely the creator's audience is to buy on their word. 1.00 is average; 1.16 means 16% more orders per click than average. Formula (simulation): trust = category base × (0.85 + sentiment ÷ 400 + (ER ÷ tier median ER − 1) × 0.06) × noise − sponsored share ÷ 400, kept between 0.6 and 1.6. Category bases run from 1.30 for doctors and dietitians and 1.20 for finance and parenting down to 0.85 for comedy and music. Example: Kunal K. is 1.16.

Why it matters. It multiplies straight into the order forecast and is 25% of conversion Performance. Good. Sample median 1.02. Say it. "Two creators can get the same clicks and very different sales. Trust is what separates them, and doctors and finance creators carry the most."

Confidence (High, Medium, Low)

Confidence and forecast band

How sure the engine is about a creator's forecast, based on how much tracked history it has. Formula: High if 5 or more tracked campaigns and at least 1 brand-lift study (band ±12%); Medium if 2 or more tracked campaigns (±22%); otherwise Low (±35%). Example: Kunal K. has 0 tracked campaigns, so he is Low. His forecast of about 23 orders reads as roughly 15 to 31 (22.9 × 0.65 = 14.9; 22.9 × 1.35 = 30.9).

Why it matters. It stops you from promising a point number. A plan's band is the spend-weighted average of its creators' bands. Good. In the sample, 95 creators are High, 179 Medium and 146 Low. The Amazon conversion plan carries ±23%. Say it. "Every forecast has a range. A creator we've tracked five times gets ±12%; one we've never tracked gets ±35%, and the profile says so."

Say it like this

"Each creator carries about twenty numbers. We compare them only within the same tier, because a 1% engagement rate means something different at 30,000 followers and at 3 million. Three questions come first: are the followers real, is the content safe, and do we have the history to trust the forecast. Everything else decides the rank."

Worked example: Kunal K.'s audience fit for Amazon

Raw audience fit = 0.30 × gender + 0.30 × age + 0.25 × city tier + 0.15 × affluence = 0.30 × 0.977 + 0.30 × 0.951 + 0.25 × 0.957 + 0.15 × 0.88 = 0.2931 + 0.2853 + 0.2393 + 0.1320 = 0.950. Why stretch the scale?

Almost every creator overlaps a broad brief by at least half, simply because most Indian Instagram audiences are 18–34. Without the stretch, a near-perfect match (0.95) and a poor one (0.60) would look close. The stretch makes 0.60 score 22 and 0.95 score 100, so the difference shows.

Say it like this

"We don't match on the creator. We match on the creator's audience. For Kunal and Amazon, the audience is 50% women against a 52% target, 95% age overlap and 96% city-tier overlap, so audience fit is 100. All of it comes from platform insights the creator consents to share."

Conversion, orders and revenue

Conversion rate

The share of clicks that become orders. Formula: orders ÷ tracked clicks. Example: 3,604 ÷ 26,348 = 13.7%. This is higher than Amazon's base click-to-order rate of 4% because tracked orders include promo-code, affiliate and search-assisted orders, not just orders from the click itself (the assist factor, see 2.7).

Orders and attributed revenue

Orders traced to the campaign through tracked links, promo codes, affiliate or marketplace attribution, and a modelled view-through share. Attributed revenue is the value of those orders. Formula: attributed revenue = Σ order value. Example: 3,604 orders and ₹68.9L (₹68,89,322), an average of ₹1,912 per order against the ₹1,900 AOV (average order value) in the brief. The split by source is links 46%, codes 22%, affiliate 21%, view-through 11%.

Returns and verified revenue

Some orders come back. Verified revenue is revenue matched to brand data and net of returns. Formula: verified revenue = attributed revenue × (1 − returns rate). Example: ₹68,89,322 × (1 − 9%) = ₹62,69,283, so ₹62.7L. Returns rates in the six simulated campaigns run from 2% (Dabur) to 17% (Meesho).

Organic baseline and incrementality share

Some buyers would have bought anyway, campaign or not. That is the organic baseline. The incrementality share is the part of verified revenue the campaign actually caused, measured with a holdout. Formula: incremental revenue = verified revenue × incrementality share; organic baseline = verified − incremental. Example: ₹62,69,283 × 62% = ₹38.9L incremental (₹38,86,955); baseline ₹23.8L (₹23,82,328). The shares used (45–66% across campaigns) are category-typical assumptions in the simulation; in practice the holdout replaces them.

EMV and claimed ROI

EMV (earned media value) puts a rupee price on every view and engagement, as if the brand had bought them as ads. It is how vanity ROI is usually quoted in the industry. Formula in this prototype: EMV = ₹1.4 × reported views + ₹28 × engagements. Example: 3,513,707 × ₹1.4 = ₹49,19,190, plus 93,206 × ₹28 = ₹26,09,768, gives EMV ₹75,28,958 (₹75.3L). Claimed ROI = ₹75.3L ÷ ₹22.4L = 3.36×. Note that EMV uses reported views, the inflated top of the funnel.

LTV and 12-month value

LTV (lifetime value) is what a customer is worth over time. The platform uses a 12-month multiplier per brand: how much a new customer buys over a year relative to the first order. Formula: LTV ROAS = iROAS × 12-month multiplier. Example: Amazon's multiplier is 1.0 (festive marketplace orders are not credited with repeat buying), so LTV ROAS stays 1.74×. Olay's is 1.5, so its 0.98× iROAS becomes 0.98 × 1.5 ≈ 1.46× (the export shows 1.46 from unrounded inputs). Meesho's is 2.2 and HK Vitals' 2.1.

Brand lift, ad recall lift and the geo holdout

Brand lift (control vs exposed)

A survey of two matched groups. The exposed group saw the campaign, the control group did not. The gap is the lift. Formula: lift = exposed % − control %, in percentage points. Example (n = 1,200 per cell): ad recall 21.0% → 31.5%, brand consideration 48.0% → 53.4%, purchase intent 36.0% → 41.8%.

Ad recall lift

The brand-lift line that asks "do you remember an ad for this brand?". Formula: exposed recall − control recall. Example: 31.5 − 21.0 = +10.5 pts. Across the six simulated campaigns the mean is +9.8 pts.

Geo holdout

A test that holds the campaign back in some cities. Six test cities get the campaign, six matched control cities do not, and you compare conversion. Formula: lift = test rate ÷ control rate − 1. Example: 2.98 vs 1.87 conversions per 1,000 TG users, so 2.98 ÷ 1.87 − 1 = +59.4%. From this the share of attributed orders that are incremental is set at 62%.

MAPE (forecast error)

MAPE

Mean absolute percentage error: on average, how far the forecast missed, ignoring direction. Formula: MAPE = average over posted creators of |actual views − forecast views| ÷ forecast views × 100. Example: Avni T. forecast 236,055 views and got 213,420, an error of 9.6%. Keerthi B. forecast 382,331 and got 441,129 (+15.4%). Across all posted Amazon creators, MAPE is 19.4%.

Good. The GTM plan sets views MAPE under 30% at v1 (days 30–45), under 25% at the day-90 scale check, and under 20% as the KPI target. A MAPE above 35% at day 45 is a named risk trigger. The six simulated campaigns run from 16.0% to 19.4%.

Say it like this

"On Amazon, the industry's usual number would be a 3.36× ROI. That's EMV: a rate card applied to views creators reported. We walk it down. Reported views to real people in the target group, attributed sales to verified sales net of 9% returns, then the holdout tells us 62% of that was caused by the campaign. What's left is 1.74× incremental ROAS. It's a smaller number, and it's the one a CFO will accept."

What "verified" means

Only L4 and L5 numbers are called verified. L0 to L3 are useful, and they are reported with their level, but the word "verified" is reserved for outcomes matched to the brand's own data. The GTM plan's guardrail says the same: forecasts carry bands, and "verified" is used only for L4 and L5.

Be realistic about reach

Holdouts and lift studies don't fit every brief. Small campaigns will stay at L3–L4. The Year 1 goal in the plan is 90% of revenue at L4 or above, and the month-6 check is L4+ covering at least 60% of revenue.

Say it like this

"Every number we put in front of a client says which level it is. L0 is a screenshot. L5 is a holdout. We only use the word 'verified' for L4 and L5, so when a brand sees it, they know it's tied to their own sales data."

2.5 The fit score

The fit score is a 0–100 number for how well one creator suits one brand brief under one objective. It only ranks creators who pass the hard gates (2.6).

Fit = Σ (part score × weight ÷ 100) + Represent boost (≤ 5)

Each of the seven parts is scored 0–100. The weights for each objective add up to 100, so a weight of 26 means that part supplies up to 26 points of the total.

Within-tier percentiles

Percentile within tier

The share of creators in the same tier with a value at or below this creator's. Formula: percentile = (number of same-tier creators with value ≤ this value) ÷ (creators in the tier). "Top X%" = (1 − percentile) × 100, at least 1. Example: Kunal's CTR of 1.58% is at or above 98.8% of the 168 Micro creators, so the profile says "top 1% of micro creators".

All Performance inputs use within-tier percentiles. This is what lets a nano creator with 4.4% ER and a mega creator with 0.4% ER compete fairly. Each is judged against its peers.

The weights are a starting point

These are expert weights for v0, set by hand. The strategist can move the sliders on the Brand Match screen and the ranking recomputes. The roadmap replaces them with learned weights: v2 (days 60–90) uses learning-to-rank on strategist picks plus outcomes, and v3 (months 4–6) recalibrates on holdout and brand-lift results.

The Represent boost

Represent boost

Represent is Chtrbox's talent-management arm, which manages an exclusive roster of creators (39 creators carry the roster flag in the sample). The strategist can add a boost so roster creators rank a little higher. Formula: boost = min(slider value, 5), added to the total only for Represent creators. Example: a 5-point boost lifts a roster creator at 78 to 83.

The cap at +5 is deliberate. It can break a tie between two good creators. It cannot lift a weak creator over a much better one, and it never overrides a gate. In the Amazon conversion plan, 5 of the 80 creators are on the roster, taking ₹8.4L of spend, with no boost applied.

Say it like this

"The fit score has seven parts. The weights change with the objective. For awareness, audience and language matter most. For conversion, click-through and trust matter most. Authenticity stays at 14% every time, because fake followers break every brief. Performance is always compared within the creator's tier. The Represent boost is capped at five points, so it settles ties and never overrides quality."

2.7 The forecast, step by step

For every creator and every brief, predict() forecasts what one standard bundle will deliver: 1 Reel + 2 Stories on Instagram, or one integration on YouTube. The steps below are the exact arithmetic.

Cost

Instagram: Reel rate + 2 × Story rate. YouTube: the YouTube integration rate.

Views

Reel views = average views. Story views = followers × tier story rate × 2 (two stories). Story rates: Nano 12%, Micro 9%, Mid-tier 7%, Macro 5.5%, Mega 4%. YouTube has no stories.

Clicks

Instagram: (Reel views × CTR × 0.3 + Story views × CTR × 1.3) ÷ 100. Story link stickers click at 1.3× the creator's CTR, Reels (link in bio) at 0.3×. YouTube: views × CTR × 0.55 ÷ 100. Then multiply by (0.55 + 0.9 × content affinity) and by the market factor (1 in market, 0.2 outside).

Orders

Clicks × brand conversion rate × creator trust × (0.7 + 0.6 × affinity) × ASSIST. ASSIST = 2.8, the promo-code, affiliate and search-assisted orders that come with each clicked order.

Revenue and ROAS

Revenue = orders × brand AOV. ROAS = revenue ÷ cost.

TG views

Views × real-follower share × TG share × market factor, where TG share = (raw audience fit)1.6, kept between 5% and 95%.

Engagements and recall

Engagements = followers × ER + story views × 3%. Recall lift = past recall × (0.55 + 0.6 × affinity) × (0.7 + 0.3 × TG share).

2.8 The plan builder

The plan builder turns a ranked list into a set of creators that fits the budget (buildPlan). It picks in order of a selection key, which is different from the fit score on purpose.

Selection key = (fit ÷ 100)2.2 × (real followers ÷ 100)6 × outcome ÷ (cost + ₹3,000 ops)
  • Only creators with fit ≥ 50 who passed every gate are candidates.
  • Fit2.2 rewards strong matches more than proportionally. A fit of 83 has (83 ÷ 60)2.2 = 2.04× the weight of a fit of 60.
  • Real followers6 punishes fake audiences hard, even above the gate. A creator at 78% real followers needs (90.9 ÷ 78)6 = 2.5× the outcome per rupee to tie with one at 90.9%.
  • Outcome is the objective's unit: TG views × (1 + recall ÷ 25) for awareness, engagements for consideration, revenue for conversion.
  • ₹3,000 ops is the cost to contract, brief, check and pay each creator. It stops the plan from filling up on tiny deals. A ₹3,000 nano bundle (₹2,000 Reel + 2 × ₹500 Stories) costs ₹6,000 once handled.

How unique reach is estimated

Unique TG reach

The number of different TG people the plan reaches, after allowing for people who see several creators. Formula: for each creator, the chance a TG person is reached = min(0.9, TG views × 0.62 ÷ TG pool). Unique reach = TG pool × (1 − Π(1 − that chance)). 0.62 is the assumed share of a creator's TG views that are distinct people; the pool is the brand's addressable TG (Amazon 330M, Olay 48M). Example: Amazon's plan has 3,390,942 TG views; × 0.62 = 2,102,384; the overlap correction across 80 creators brings it to 2,095,948. Olay's smaller pool means more overlap: 1,342,998 × 0.62 = 832,659 becomes 825,762.

The family that compounds

Outcomes is the family competitors can't buy. Every campaign adds a forecast-versus-actual pair for each creator, and from L4 an outcome tied to a brand's sales. That is what moves a creator from Low to High confidence and what the v1 to v3 models train on.

Say it like this

"The engine reads 76 data points per creator in eight families: who they are, who watches, how they create, what they deliver, whether the audience is real, whether the brand is safe, what it costs, and what actually happened.
