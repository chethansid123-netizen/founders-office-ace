Part 1

Understand it first

This part explains what the Creator Intelligence Engine is, the problem it solves, how it works stage by stage, and how it was built. It walks one Amazon festive brief through the whole engine using the platform's own numbers. Every chapter ends with the words to say out loud.

(a) What it is

In one sentence

The Creator Intelligence Engine is software that picks the right creators for a brand brief, forecasts what they will deliver, and then proves what they actually delivered, using data instead of follower counts.

In one paragraph

Every brand that hires Chtrbox asks three questions: who should we work with, what will it deliver, and did it work? Today a strategist answers the first by hand, the second by experience, and the third with a wrap-up deck full of reported views. The engine answers all three with data. It holds 76 data points per creator in eight families (identity, audience, content, performance, authenticity, safety, commercials, outcomes). For each brief it removes creators who fail hard checks (fake audience, brand-safety risk, a recent competitor post, regulatory eligibility), ranks the rest with a seven-part fit score that shows its reasons, builds a plan under the budget, and forecasts views, clicks, orders and recall with a confidence band. After the campaign it plugs into the tracking Chtrbox already runs and reports every number on a six-level ladder from "reported" (L0) to "incremental" (L5). Each campaign's forecast error then retrains the model.

In one page

The prototype is one offline HTML file with seven screens. It runs on a sample of 420 synthetic creators, 35 brands (30 publicly reported Chtrbox clients and 5 labelled prospects) across 9 verticals, and 6 simulated campaigns.

The headline numbers on the Overview, all simulated: a median of 14% of creators are gated out per brief before any ranking; ₹1.13 Cr incremental revenue across the 6 campaigns (₹11,299,955, the sum of each campaign's incremental revenue); 1.33× incremental ROAS on the five non-awareness campaigns (1.95× on 12-month customer value); and a mean ad-recall lift of +9.8 points across the 6 lift studies.

ROAS and iROAS

ROAS is return on ad spend: revenue divided by spend. iROAS (incremental ROAS) keeps only the revenue a holdout test shows would not have happened anyway. Formula: iROAS = incremental revenue ÷ spend. Example: the Amazon campaign has ₹38.9L incremental revenue on ₹22.4L spend, so iROAS = 1.74×.

The analogy: a selection committee with ball-by-ball data

Think of a cricket selection committee picking an XI for a Test in Chennai. A lazy committee picks the eleven most famous names. A good one asks what this pitch needs (spin or pace), checks fitness before anyone is considered, compares each player's form against others in the same role, estimates what each will score, and after the match reads the scorecard to decide the next selection.

The engine does the same thing for creators. The brief is the pitch: awareness, consideration and conversion need different players, which is why the fit weights change by objective. The hard gates are the fitness test. Comparing performance within tier is comparing form within role, so a nano creator is judged against nano creators. The forecast is the expected score. The verified-ROI report is the scorecard, and the learning loop is the next selection meeting. A follower-count plan is the committee that picks the eleven most famous names.

It fits because every leader in the room already understands why fame alone picks bad teams, and because it carries the full loop of select, predict, verify and learn in one picture.

(b) The problem from first principles

What a brand pays for is a change in what people think or do: remembering the brand, considering it, or buying it. Views, likes and follower counts are proxies for that change. Influencer marketing still runs on the weakest proxies because they are the easiest to collect.

Why vanity metrics survive

Information asymmetry

Creators report their own numbers, often as screenshots. A brand cannot check how many followers are real. Example: in the Amazon campaign, creators reported 3.51M views; the API confirmed 2.90M.

No counterfactual

A counterfactual is what would have happened without the campaign. Few campaigns run a holdout, so nobody knows which sales would have come anyway during a festive sale. Example: of Amazon's ₹62.7L verified revenue, the model treats 62% as incremental, so about ₹23.8L would have happened anyway (₹62.7L × 0.38).

Fragmented data

Reach lives on Instagram, clicks in link tools, orders in the brand's systems, returns in the warehouse. Nobody joins them, so nobody can say "this creator caused these orders, net of returns".

Incentives

Bigger reported numbers make the creator, the agency and the brand manager all look good in the wrap-up deck. Nobody in the chain is paid to report the smaller, honest number.

Engagement rate (ER) and CTR

ER is interactions divided by followers. CTR (click-through rate) is link clicks divided by views. Formula: ER = (likes + comments + saves + shares) ÷ followers; CTR = clicks ÷ views. Example: Kunal K. has ER 3.45% and link CTR 1.58%, top 1% of micro creators.

(e) How the engine works: six stages

The Overview shows six stages. Each takes something in and hands something on. In the prototype, stages 1 and 2 are simulated by the data generator; stages 3 and 4 are real code running in the browser; stages 5 and 6 are shown on simulated campaigns.

Ingest

In: creator platform data through consent (Instagram Graph, YouTube Analytics), Chtrbox's campaign history, the Represent roster, brand outcome feeds (pixels, UTMs, codes, marketplace data), and the brand brief. Out: one record per creator with 76 fields, each with a source and a timestamp.

Understand

In: captions, audio and video frames, comments, and the brief. What happens: vision and language models tag niche, cultural codes (such as "Festive & occasions" or "Value-seeker"), language (12 languages, Hinglish-aware), comment sentiment and brand-safety risk. The brief becomes a Brand DNA: target gender, age, city tier, affluence, languages, codes, competitors and compliance class. Out: tagged creators and a structured brief.

Match

In: Brand DNA and creator records. What happens: six hard gates first (real followers below 72%, safety below 55, a competitor post in the last 60 days, SEBI or health-credential eligibility, wrong market), then a seven-part fit score weighted by objective. Out: a ranked list where every score shows its parts and its reasons, plus a gated list with the reason for each exclusion.

Forecast

In: each eligible creator's views, story reach, CTR, trust, rate card, plus the brand's conversion rate and order value. What happens: the per-creator forecast formulas below, then a plan builder that fills the budget. Out: views, target-group views, clicks, orders, revenue, recall and cost per outcome, each with a confidence band; a plan; and a follower-count plan at the same spend for comparison.

Verify

In: live data from Chtrbox's existing tracking and attribution (links, codes, affiliate), API-pulled views, brand order and returns data, holdout cities and lift surveys. What happens: deduplicate, bot-filter, keep the target group, net out returns, subtract the organic baseline. Out: a report where every number carries its level, L0 to L5.

Learn

In: forecast-versus-actual pairs for every creator, and every strategist override with a reason. What happens: forecasts are recalibrated and the ranking is retrained. Out: a better model for the next brief. On the 6 sample campaigns the views forecast error (MAPE) is 16.0% to 19.4%.

Each part is scored 0 to 100. Its contribution is part score × weight ÷ 100, because the weights add up to 100. The Represent boost is off by default, capped at 5 points, and shown on the score when used.

The forecast, per creator

Views = average reel views + story views (followers × tier story-view rate × 2 stories)
Target-group views = views × real-follower share × target-group share
Clicks = (reel views × CTR × 0.3 + story views × CTR × 1.3) × (0.55 + 0.9 × category affinity)
Orders = clicks × brand conversion rate × creator trust × (0.7 + 0.6 × affinity) × 2.8 assist factor

The assist factor of 2.8 counts orders that come through codes, affiliate links and later searches for every order that comes from a direct click. It is a modelling assumption to be calibrated in days 31 to 45. The confidence band is ±12% for creators with 5+ tracked campaigns and a lift study, ±22% for 2+ campaigns, and ±35% otherwise.

The plan builder

The plan builder considers creators with fit of 50 or more. It ranks them by fit2.2 × (real-follower share)6 × outcome ÷ (cost + ₹3,000 operating cost per creator), then fills the budget with caps of one mega and three macro creators, no creator above 25% of the budget, and at most 80 creators. The steep power on real-follower share means a creator with 70% real followers scores far below one with 90%. The follower-count plan for comparison simply buys the biggest accounts in the market until the same budget runs out.

Say it like this

"The engine has six stages: ingest, understand, match, forecast, verify, learn. The important design choice is that the hard checks come before any score, so a creator with fake followers or a competitor post from last month never even gets ranked. The score itself is seven parts with weights that change by objective, and every creator shows why they ranked where they did. Nothing is a black box; a strategist can see the parts and override them."

(g) What is real and what is simulated

Leadership will ask this in the first five minutes. Know the split cold.

Real

  • The engine logic: gates, the seven-part score, the forecast formulas, the plan builder, the ROI ladder. It runs live in the browser.
  • Brand names: 30 publicly reported Chtrbox clients. The other 5 (Tata 1mg, Cult, Groww, PolicyBazaar, CRED) are labelled prospects.
  • Market benchmarks the data is calibrated to: tier ER medians and published rate ranges for India.
  • Rules: ASCI disclosure and Addendum II credentials, SEBI finfluencer rules, the DPDP Rules.
  • Chtrbox's public company figures, per public coverage of the filings.

Simulated

  • All 420 creators: synthetic personas with generated names and IDs (CIE-1001 onwards).
  • Every creator metric: followers, views, ER, CTR, audience, rate card, collaborations.
  • Every brand metric: order values, conversion rates, targets, budgets.
  • The 6 campaigns and their results, including incrementality shares (45–66%) and return rates (2–17%), which are typical-of-category assumptions.
  • The assist factor of 2.8.

Why the creators are synthetic

Two reasons. First, putting invented metrics on real creators would be wrong: it attaches false numbers to real people's names, and a fake-follower score on a real creator is a serious claim. Second, Chtrbox's team knows real creator numbers. If you showed a real creator with a made-up ER, someone in the room would know it is wrong and stop trusting everything else on the screen. Synthetic personas keep the conversation on the logic, which is what you want them to judge.

The data is calibrated so it looks like the market. The sample's own medians match the targets: nano 4.39% ER, micro 2.82%, mid 1.35%, macro 0.81%, mega 0.33%.

Never say "Amazon got 1.74×"

Say "the simulated Amazon campaign shows 1.74×". The brand name is real; the number is not. Say it once clearly at the start and once more when you show Campaigns & ROI.

Say it like this

"Before I start: the engine logic is real and runs live, but the creators are synthetic personas and every metric is simulated. The brand names are your publicly reported clients, but none of their numbers are real. I did that on purpose. Putting invented numbers on real creators would be wrong, and you know real creator numbers better than any dataset, so you'd spot it immediately. I calibrated it to India's 2025–26 benchmarks so it behaves like the market. With your campaign history, the same logic runs on real data."

(i) How it was built

The prototype

gen_data.py makes the data

A Python script with a fixed random seed (20261021), so the data is the same every time. It creates 420 synthetic creators, 35 brands and 6 campaigns, and writes data.json.

build_html.py bundles one file

It merges the HTML template, the stylesheet, app.js, the data and three fonts (Bricolage Grotesque, Geist, Geist Mono, embedded as base64) into one HTML file that runs offline in any browser. No server, no internet, no install.

app.js is the engine and the screens

Everything runs in the browser: the scoring functions (audFit, affOf, cultFit, perfOf, authOf, safeOf), predict, the gates, scoreBrand, buildPlan, vanityPlan, and all seven screens. Charts (line, scatter, sparkline, waterfall, ROI dots) are drawn as SVG by hand with no chart library.

The PDF is generated from the engine

export_engine.mjs opens the built page in headless Chromium, calls scoreBrand, buildPlan and vanityPlan for every brand, and saves the results. build_pdf.py builds the 36-page GTM plan from that export, and render_pdf.mjs prints it to A4. The numbers in the PDF and on screen come from the same code.

  • Rate cards scale with followers, tier, engagement and category (finance ×1.35, tech ×1.25, health ×1.2), landing in published India ranges (micro ₹5K–50K, mid ₹50K–2L, macro ₹2–5L a reel). A story costs 30% of a reel, a YouTube video 1.6× a reel, and UAE creators 1.6× more.
  • Larger accounts carry more fake followers. The fraud rate rises from 6% (nano) to 16% (mega). A fraud-flagged creator gets 52–71% real followers, 38% lower engagement, and a one-month follower spike of 22–48%, which is what the authenticity checks look for. 32 of 420 creators are flagged.
  • Culture is modelled. 16 categories, 32 cities including Dubai, 18 cultural codes, credentials, and past brand collaborations with day counts that drive the competitor gate.

Suggested stack (a recommendation, to confirm with the tech lead)

Ingestion: Python workers on a scheduler (Airflow or Dagster) calling the Instagram Graph and YouTube Analytics APIs, plus connectors for tracking and brand feeds. Storage: Postgres for the app; a warehouse (BigQuery or Snowflake) with dbt for the 76-field creator tables, hosted in an India region for data residency; object storage for media. AI layer: a hosted vision-language model for tagging and safety review, speech-to-text for audio, and a multilingual model fine-tuned on Hinglish comments for sentiment. Models: gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM) for forecasts with calibrated intervals, LightGBM's ranking mode for learning-to-rank, open-source geo-experiment tools (such as GeoLift) for holdouts, and MLflow to version models. Apps: React front end, FastAPI back end, and a BI tool for brand dashboards. Build what is defensible (the model, the ROI pipeline, the tool); buy commodity creator data and survey panels at first.

Say it like this

"The prototype is one offline HTML file. A Python script generates 420 synthetic creators calibrated to Indian benchmarks, and the whole engine runs in the browser: the scoring, the forecast, the plan builder, even the charts. The PDF plan is generated by running that same engine, so every number in the document matches the screen. For production, the four things to build are consented data ingestion starting with Represent, an AI layer that reads Hinglish and regional content, models that get calibrated on your real campaigns, and the brand-facing report. It maps onto 90 days: a ranked list by day 15, the first verified-ROI report by day 45, and half of campaigns at L4 or above by day 90."
