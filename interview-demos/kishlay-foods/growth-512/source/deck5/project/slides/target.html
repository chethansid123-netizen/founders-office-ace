TESTING THE TARGET (90 s). “Before building a plan to ₹512 Cr, I tested the number itself. I couldn’t find it in any public source — the only public ‘512’ is Tulsi’s headcount in February 2026. And the starting point has moved: the FY25 filing shows ₹288 Cr, down 16%. From ₹288 Cr, ₹512 Cr needs 33% a year to FY27, 21% to FY28, 15.5% to FY29, 12% to FY30 and 10% to FY31 — the yellow band is the category’s 8–12%, and the green line is Kishlay’s 5.3% a year from FY21 to FY24. Five tests for FY29. One, growth: 15.5% a year from FY25, or 18% from my FY26 estimate — about twice the category. Only the bull case does that. Two, track record: FY25 fell 16%, the three years before grew 5.3% a year, and management’s own guidance to CRISIL in 2024 was ₹350–400 Cr. Stretch. Three, capacity: ₹512 Cr is 1,940–2,840 tonnes a month at ₹150–220 a kilo, against 2,600 installed with the two 3P partners; FY25 used roughly 42–62%. So it needs richer mix or more 3P. Four, market room: even at ₹512 Cr Kishlay is under 1% of a ₹61,000–64,000 Cr market. Pass. Five, profit: ₹51 Cr of EBITDA is 2.7 times my FY26 estimate — and Annapurna already earns 11.6% at ₹520 Cr. There’s a precedent. FY27 fails outright: +78% in two years. So my verdict: commit to about ₹460 Cr and ₹46 Cr of EBITDA for FY29 — that’s the base case — keep ₹512 Cr as the stretch, which the bull case reaches in FY29 and the base case in FY31, and steer by EBITDA, because listed peers are valued at about 9 times EBITDA but only about 1 times revenue. And my first question to you: where does 512 come from, and which year do you have in mind?” Engagement: ask it and listen. If they insist on FY29, go to slide 6: the ₹54 Cr gap closes only with the bull-case engines, an ~₹80 Cr acquisition, or one more year. Transition: “Here is how the engines add up.”
