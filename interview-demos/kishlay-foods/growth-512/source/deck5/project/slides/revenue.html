THE REVENUE BRIDGE (75 s). “Here is the plan built bottom-up. I start from FY25’s actual ₹288 Cr and assume an 8% recovery in FY26 to about ₹311 Cr — Tulsi’s headcount was up 15% year on year by February, which suggests volumes are coming back; that’s the first number I’d check. The general-trade core grows 4.5% a year, below its 5.3% history because I assume tail SKUs get cut, and adds about ₹40 Cr. Quick commerce adds about ₹14 Cr: 1,000 dark stores in the East and Northeast, six SKUs, one unit per SKU per store per day at ₹62 net. Modern trade and online add about ₹13 Cr. Exports add about ₹31 Cr: Bhutan on its small base, Nepal at eight trucks a month, and the GCC at six containers a month from FY28 — only if the shipping-route test passes. Co-packing on spare lines adds ₹17 Cr, and one ₹30 Cr bolt-on bought in mid-FY28 adds ₹33 Cr. That’s ₹458 Cr in FY29, or ₹425 Cr without the bolt-on — about ₹54 Cr short of ₹512 Cr. To close it by FY29 you’d need one of: general trade growing 10% a year instead of 4.5%, an acquisition of about ₹80 Cr of revenue instead of ₹30 Cr — roughly a ₹95 Cr cheque, which this balance sheet can’t carry — about 4,000 more dark stores, or simply more time: about ₹504 Cr in FY30 and past ₹512 Cr in FY31. The bear case is ₹338 Cr; the bull case ₹564 Cr. Every driver is an input in the model.” Engagement: “Which of these drivers would you challenge first?” Transition: “Revenue is half the problem. Margin is the other half.”
