THE MARGIN BRIDGE (75 s). “Kishlay doesn’t publish EBITDA — the FY25 filing shows only a net loss of ₹6.31 Cr, about −2.2% — so I start from an estimate of about 6% EBITDA, the first number I’d confirm with finance. Getting to 10% needs about 400 basis points. The biggest lever, 140 points, is mix: more kilos in quick commerce, modern trade, exports and bigger packs — that’s slide three in action. Second, 100 points from fuller plants: FY25’s output looks like 40–60% of the 2,600 tonnes a month across the own plant and the two 3P partners, so co-packing and more volume spread the fixed costs. Then 70 from cutting the tail of 236 items, 60 from procurement on potato, oil, flour and packaging film, 60 from paying trade schemes on what sells out rather than what we push in, and 40 from freight, using the Bihar and Chhattisgarh 3P lines to serve nearby markets. Against that, I take 50 points off for quick-commerce fees and ads, and 30 for people, MIS and brand — growth isn’t free. A bolt-on bought at 10% or better adds about 10. That lands at 10% — from about ₹18.7 Cr of EBITDA in FY26 to about ₹45.8 Cr in FY29. The levers ramp 30% in FY27, 65% in FY28 and fully in FY29. Mix and fuller plants are 60% of the gain, which is why the two numbers I’d track from week one are contribution per kilo and utilisation by line.” Engagement: “Which lever do you think has the most room — procurement or trade spend?” Transition: “Now the three engines, starting with quick commerce.”
