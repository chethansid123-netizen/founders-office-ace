ENGINE 3: ASSET-LIGHT CAPACITY AND M&A (75 s). “The third engine needs no new market. Kishlay’s own hub in Guwahati is about 2,000 tonnes a month, and two 3P partners — AFP in Hajipur, Bihar, and GUD in Chhattisgarh — add about 300 each. That’s 2,600 tonnes. If Kishlay realises ₹150–220 a kilo — my assumption — FY25’s ₹288 Cr means 1,100–1,600 tonnes of output a month, so 1,000–1,500 tonnes a month are spare. Frito-Lay and Britannia already buy co-packing from Kishlay; renewing both and winning two more contracts adds about ₹17 Cr by FY29. Then one bolt-on: a regional brand of about ₹30 Cr revenue. At 1.0–1.5× sales it costs ₹30–45 Cr; my base case is 1.2×, about ₹36 Cr. Precedents: Prataap bought Avadh in 2018 at an implied ₹185 Cr; Advent bought DFM, the Crax company, in 2019 at about ₹1,252 Cr; Bikaji bought 55% of Ariba in 2023, implying about ₹110 Cr. The screen: ₹20–80 Cr revenue, at least 10% EBITDA, a new state or category, a plant within 600 km of a depot, and a clean GST and FSSAI record. Three guardrails. AFP is a partner and a competitor, so recipes and price lists need protecting. Bank limits ran at 93%, so the deal should be funded with equity, an earn-out or a seller note — not working capital. And the CRISIL reporting needs fixing first, because lenders and sellers will read it. Funnel by week 12: twenty screened, five meetings, one term sheet.” Engagement: “Have you looked at any brands since the Britannia conversations?” — only if they raise it; otherwise ask, “Which regions would you want a bolt-on to open?” Transition: “Here’s how the 90 days run.”
