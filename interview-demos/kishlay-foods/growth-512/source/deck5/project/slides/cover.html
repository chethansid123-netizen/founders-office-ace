OPENING HOOK (60 s). Pause, then: “One kilo of snacks. In ₹10 kirana packs it earns Kishlay about ₹34 after raw material, packing, freight and schemes. Put the same kilo in a 400-gram pack on Blinkit and it earns about ₹54. Put it on a Dubai shelf and it earns about ₹99. Those are my estimates from public prices — the method is in the Excel model, and the first thing I would do in the job is replace them with your real numbers. But the direction is the answer to the problem I was given: how Tulsi Speciality Foods breaks out of the ₹5/₹10 trap and reaches ₹512 Cr at about 10% EBITDA. Here is where the business stands. The latest filed numbers, via Tracxn, show ₹288 Cr of revenue in FY25 — down 16% from ₹343 Cr — and a net loss of ₹6.31 Cr. CRISIL’s last consolidated figures were ₹335.59 Cr and a loss of ₹11.50 Cr in FY23. Raw materials are 70–75% of cost. My answer in one line: not more packs — more money per kilo. Three engines do it: multipacks on quick commerce and modern trade, asset-light capacity plus one bolt-on, and exports — nearest first. I’ll test whether ₹512 Cr is even the right number, build it bottom-up, and end with a 90-day plan that has a budget, owners and stop rules.” Engagement: ask, “Before I start — is ₹512 Cr your number for FY29, or is it a different year?” Then move on. Transition: “Let me start with where a ₹10 pack’s money goes.”
