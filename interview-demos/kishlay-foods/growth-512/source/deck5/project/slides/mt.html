ENGINE 1B: MODERN TRADE — HOW TO BREAK IN (90 s). “Modern trade is where the 200- and 400-gram packs earn their keep — about ₹49 a kilo against ₹34 in ₹10 packs — so here is exactly how I’d get in. Where first: Vishal Mega Mart. It has about 72 stores in the Northeast, 44 to 60 of them in Assam, FMCG is about 28% of its sales, and about 35% of that FMCG is its own label. That last fact is our way in: we can offer to co-pack Vishal’s own-label snacks from Tulsi’s plant, which builds the relationship and fills our lines, and then list Non Stop alongside. In parallel, five to ten independent Guwahati supermarkets — JDDS, Prince, The Grocer, Nayantara and others — list fast with low or no fees, so they’re our pilot doors. Kolkata comes next: Spencer’s, with 131 stores and a stated focus on East India; then More, Reliance Smart Bazaar in Siliguri and Kolkata, and Arambagh’s. DMart comes later; its growth is in the north and west. How: a clean vendor kit — FSSAI, GST, GS1 barcodes, MRP labels, at least six months of shelf life; a pitch built on a gap the chains have — a Northeast shelf of Jolokia chanachur, Jhalmuri and 40 Aana — at the same ₹55 and ₹110 prices as Haldiram’s and Bikaji, with next-day supply from Guwahati that national brands can’t match. Terms to plan for: about a 20% margin, listing fees of roughly ₹2,500 to ₹5,000 per SKU where chains charge them, and 7 to 60 days of credit. Use a modern-trade distributor at 3–5% until volumes justify the chains’ warehouses. Then sell-out: eye level, weekend sampling, weekly data from the chains’ vendor portals, and a 95% fill rate — chains drop unreliable suppliers. One door, in numbers: at ₹33,000 of sales a month — about 15 packs a day — a door earns about ₹8,600 of contribution; the pilot spends ₹44,000 a door on listing, promoters and sampling, so it pays back in about five months, and break-even is about ₹12,900 a month. The rollout is 10 doors in the pilot, 60 by the end of FY27, 150 by FY28 and 400 by FY29 — about ₹15.8 Cr plus ₹3 Cr online. The catch is cash: at 45 days’ credit, 400 doors tie up about ₹2 Cr of receivables, so we start with chains that pay fast and gate every stage on ₹33,000 a door.” Engagement: “Which chains already stock Kishlay today, and on what terms?” Transition: “Second engine: exports.”
