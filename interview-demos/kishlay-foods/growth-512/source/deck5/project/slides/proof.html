PROOF (60 s). “Is any of this realistic for a ₹5-pack company in the East? Look at Annapurna Swadisht from Kolkata. In FY24 it was ₹266 Cr — smaller than Kishlay’s ₹344 Cr. In FY25 it reached ₹409 Cr while Kishlay’s filing shows ₹288 Cr. By FY26 it reported ₹520 Cr, with EBITDA of about 11.6% and net profit of about ₹30 Cr, against Kishlay’s FY25 loss of ₹6.31 Cr. Annapurna’s figures come from results aggregators; I’d verify them against the NSE filing. Kishlay’s FY26 bar is dashed because it is my estimate. On the right, the margin ladder. Bikaji earned 13.7% in FY26. Annapurna 11.6%. Gopal 6.7%. Prataap, the Yellow Diamond company, 2.9% in FY25. Kishlay’s FY29 target of 10% sits in the middle — ambitious but not heroic. And scale isn’t the explanation: Gopal is about five times Kishlay’s size and earns 6.7%. My hypothesis is that mix, pricing and how full the plants run explain the gap — which is exactly what the next slides work on.” Engagement: “Do you see Annapurna in your markets — Bengal, Bihar, the Northeast?” Transition: “So is ₹512 Cr the right target?
