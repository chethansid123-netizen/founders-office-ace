THE PROBLEM (75 s). “Kishlay has done the hard part: two lakh-plus shops, three plants, a pack for every occasion. But follow ₹10. About 48 paise is GST — that’s the real 5% rate since September 2025. About ₹1.60 goes to the retailer and about 70 paise to the stockist and distributor; those are industry-typical margins, my estimates. So Kishlay receives about ₹7.22. Raw material and packing take about ₹4.91 — CRISIL says raw materials are 70–75% of cost, and that’s the part that swings with potato and oil prices. Labour, power, freight and schemes take another ₹1.23. What’s left is about ₹1.08 a pack. That’s my estimate, and the first number I’d check with finance. Second panel: growth. Revenue went from ₹173 Cr in FY19 to ₹343 Cr in FY24 — Motilal Oswal’s series — but the growth rate fell every year: 38%, 24%, 9%, 5%, 2%. Then FY25, from the company’s own filing via Tracxn: ₹288 Cr, down 16%, with a net loss of ₹6.31 Cr. CRISIL showed losses of ₹20.69 Cr in FY22 and ₹11.50 Cr in FY23 on a consolidated basis. Third panel: credit. The rating went from BBB-minus in 2021 to B-stable in June 2026, with ‘Issuer Not Cooperating’ since 2025 — CRISIL stopped receiving information. Secured charges now total about ₹70 Cr — ₹45.9 Cr on Kishlay Foods and ₹24.5 Cr on Tulsi — against ₹50 Cr of rated facilities in 2024; bank limits ran at 93% in 2023; and Tulsi shows five GST filing delays in FY26. Several of these are reporting problems, and reporting problems can be fixed in weeks.” Engagement: “Is ₹1 a pack close to what you see internally? And was FY25’s drop a real fall in sales, or did some billing move to Tulsi?” Transition: “So the question isn’t how to sell more packs — it’s where a kilo earns more.”
