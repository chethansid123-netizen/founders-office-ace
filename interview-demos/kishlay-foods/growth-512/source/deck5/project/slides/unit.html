THE INSIGHT (75 s). “This is the slide the whole plan hangs on. I took one kilo of product and asked what it earns in each channel after tax, trade margins, raw material, conversion, freight and schemes. In ₹10 kirana packs: about ₹34 a kilo — call that 1×. In a 400-gram pack in modern trade, about ₹49 — 1.4×. On quick commerce, about ₹54 — 1.6× — because there’s no retailer; the platform takes about 22% and ads about 6%, and it still wins. In the UAE, about ₹99 — 2.9×. Why? Haldiram’s 200-gram bhujia sells at AED 5.69 in Carrefour, about ₹146, against ₹55 in India; even if Kishlay prices 31% below Haldiram’s, the kilo earns three times the kirana kilo. And Nepal is the surprise: a 30% duty and 13% VAT push export to about ₹19 — below the ₹10 pack. That’s why Haldiram’s and Bikaji both now manufacture inside Nepal. A local licence earns only about ₹11 a kilo, but it needs no capital. So the strategy is one line: move kilos up this chart. One thousand tonnes moved from ₹10 packs to quick commerce adds about ₹2 Cr of contribution; to the UAE, about ₹6.5 Cr. All of these are estimates built from public prices; the formulas are open in the model, and I’d replace every input with your costing in week one.” Engagement: “Which of these channels makes the most per kilo for you today?” Transition: “Has anyone like Kishlay actually done this?”
