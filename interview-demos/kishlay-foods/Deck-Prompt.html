The prompt behind the deck

One prompt. Nine slides.
Any company.

This is the prompt that produced “Kishlay Foods, Decoded”: research first, a first-principles model second, a nine-slide story third, design last. Swap the words in braces for your next company.

Deck-Prompt.md
You are a strategy consultant and a presentation designer. Tomorrow I have a Founder's Office interview at {COMPANY} ({WEBSITE}). Build me a 9-slide, first-principles teardown of the company that I will present in the interview, plus a playbook for presenting it.

INPUTS
- My research files: {ATTACH YOUR PDFs / NOTES}
- The company's website: {WEBSITE}
- Visual references I like: {ATTACH SCREENSHOTS}
- Brand mark to use on the deck: {e.g. the Kishlay logo}
- Leave out: {e.g. funding rounds and valuation}

STEP 1. RESEARCH BEFORE YOU DESIGN
1. Read every attached file in full. Extract every number with its source.
2. Go through the website page by page (home, about, every product category, gallery, careers, contact). Record product counts per category, milestones, plant capacities, reach, certifications and every claim. Download the real images: logo, sub-brand logos, product packs, plant and office photos, mascot.
3. Audit the website twice, once as a shopper and once as a distributor, at 1440 px and on an iPhone-sized screen. Take screenshots. Measure page weight and request count. Look for broken images, mismatched claims and dates, missing prices, sizes and buy links, duplicate or misspelt products, the page title, meta description and H1.
4. Search the web for: category market size (quote a range when sources disagree), the latest revenue and EBITDA margin of 2 or 3 listed peers, recent tax changes on the category, quick commerce in the company's home city, court cases and trademark disputes, and recent news. Cite every source.
5. Keep facts and my estimates apart. Never invent a number. Label estimates "est." and keep the working for the playbook.

STEP 2. BUILD THE MODEL FROM FIRST PRINCIPLES
- Why should this company exist at all? Use the physics and economics of the product: value per truckload, freight route, shelf life and climate, fixed price points.
- Follow one unit (for example a ₹10 pack) from MRP down to what the company keeps: tax, retailer margin, trade margin, inputs, overheads, EBITDA. Compare what peers keep from the same unit.
- Compare capacity with revenue to estimate utilisation.
- Divide revenue by outlets to get sales per outlet. Compare with a peer.
- Choose ONE north-star metric and size the upside of improving it.

STEP 3. TELL IT AS A STORY: EXACTLY 9 SLIDES, FROM ZERO TO ONE
1. Hook (cover): the sharpest number in the whole analysis, in one line, plus the thesis.
2. Why the company exists: the need gap from first principles (today → the company's bridge → the ideal).
3. What it built: portfolio, price-pack ladder, brands, products by category.
4. Make: raw materials, plants, capacity, quality, estimated utilisation.
5. Move: every hop from plant to shop, and the unit waterfall.
6. Sell: the shelf moment, customer vs consumer, the purchase funnel, 3 shopper personas.
7. Fight: competitor tiers and a peer-economics table.
8. Brand and digital: brand pyramid, brand architecture, the website audit with an annotated screenshot and the fix.
9. The plan: north-star metric, four moves over 30/60/90 days and 12 months, and what I would own in the role.
Every slide answers the question the previous slide raises.

Rules for every slide:
- The title is a full sentence that states the insight (an action title), at most two lines.
- One idea and one main visual per slide.
- A chapter label above the title ("04 · Move"); the title sits in the same place on every slide.
- A footer with sources and the page number ("05 / 09").
- Speaker notes of 150–250 words: the exact words to say, the number to land, and the sentence that hands over to the next slide.

STEP 4. DESIGN
- Put the brand mark on the cover. Build the palette from the brand: one dark, one light, two accents. A dark cover, alternating light backgrounds, and a closing slide in the accent colour.
- A characterful display face for titles (for example Bricolage Grotesque) and a clean sans for text (for example Inter). One type scale (for example 96 / 64 / 40 / 28 / 24 px on a 1920 × 1080 canvas); nothing smaller than 24 px.
- Infographics instead of bullets: a price-pack staircase, horizontal bar chart, schematic map, hop chain with arrows, waterfall, narrowing funnel, persona cards with real packs, tier cards with a comparison table, pyramid, annotated screenshot with numbered pins, 4-phase plan cards, big-number tiles.
- Use only real photos and packs from the company's website. No stock photos, emoji or clip art.
- Motion: elements rise in, staged, on arrival; the hook numbers count up; fade between slides.
- Check every slide at full size: nothing overlaps, nothing runs into the footer, no word breaks mid-word, text contrast is readable.

STEP 5. DELIVER
1. The deck in Claude Slides, so I can present it and export an editable PowerPoint and a PDF.
2. The same deck as a .pptx (with speaker notes) and as a .pdf.
3. A playbook: the story spine, a talk track per slide with timings, delivery rules, a numbers cheat sheet with sources, the questions they are likely to ask with crisp answers, a guesstimate I may be asked, things to know but not lead with, and a checklist for tonight.
4. The 4 sharpest questions for me to ask the interviewer, with why each one works.

TONE
Confident, specific and respectful. Present the website and brand issues as quick wins I would own, not as criticism. Say "my estimate" whenever a number is mine, and invite correction.

Why each part is there

Research before design

Decks go generic when design starts first. Reading the files, the website and peers first gives every slide a real number.

Facts vs estimates

Interviewers test numbers. Labelling “est.” and keeping the working lets you defend or update any figure on the spot.

One unit, followed end to end

The ₹10 waterfall turns an abstract P&L into something you can say in one breath, and it pays off the hook.

Exactly 9 slides

Nine forces one idea per slide and fits a 10–12 minute slot, leaving time for the conversation that wins the job.

Action titles

A title that states the insight lets the room follow the story even if they only read titles.

Speaker notes as script

The talk track lives with the slide, so the PPTX doubles as your rehearsal script.

Real photos only

The company’s own packs and plant make the deck feel like theirs, and show you did the legwork.

Quick wins, not criticism

A website audit framed as “I’d own this” shows initiative without putting the founder on the defensive.

The 60-second version

Act as a strategy consultant and presentation designer. Research {COMPANY} ({WEBSITE}) and my attached files, audit its website on desktop and mobile, and benchmark 2–3 listed peers. Then build a 9-slide first-principles teardown (hook, need gap, portfolio, make, move with a ₹10 waterfall, sell, competition, brand and website audit, 90-day plan) in Claude Slides using the company’s logo, real product photos, brand colours, infographics instead of bullets, action titles, sources and speaker notes on every slide. Leave out {TOPICS}. Also give me a presenting playbook and the 4 best questions to ask the interviewer.
