WHAT WE SELL (90 seconds). “Twelve SKUs, each with one job, and only eight launch in the test stores. Anchors: Bikaneri and Paneer Bhujia in 400 g at ₹110, and Aloo Bhujia 200 g. They are the planner mother’s basket and they sell the most: about 12 a week each per store, about ₹5,700 a month each, and they leave about ₹45 a kilo. Regional heroes: Jolokia and Jhalmuri now, 40 Aana in wave 2. These are what national brands cannot copy quickly, an Assam and Bengal taste. Kids: Rings 12 × ₹5 with water toys and Noodles 6 × ₹10 at the child’s eye level. They earn the most per kilo, ₹82 and ₹62, because extruded snacks use cheap inputs. Bulk: a new 1 kg family pack at ₹249. It answers the per-gram shopper and raises the bill size without cutting the 400 g price. A party box follows in wave 2. Premium: a 500 g festive tin at ₹199 for Bihu, Puja and Diwali, on top shelves and end caps. Per store, the plan is about 386 packs and 116 kilos a month, about ₹32,000 at MRP, which leaves about ₹48 a kilo. That is about 8% of a Vishal Assam store’s snack sales, using JM Financial’s FMCG figure and my 8% snack-share assumption. Shelf life is 4 to 9 months, and we deliver with at least two-thirds left. Right side: per gram, we are 10% dearer than Haldiram’s and 3% dearer than Vishal’s own label. The 1 kg pack matches Haldiram’s per gram. And the raw-material plan: for the modern-trade range alone, about 17 tonnes of gram flour, 19 of palm oil and 17 of fresh potato in FY29, about three times that by FY31, bought at harvest and on contracts.” Engagement: “Which of these are in production today, and is a 1 kg pouch possible on our current lines?” Transition: “Now, where do we put them first?”
