WHAT WE SELL (90 seconds). “Twelve SKUs, each with one job, and only eight launch in the test stores; the grey rows come after the gate or in festival months. Anchors: Bikaneri and Paneer Bhujia in 400 g at ₹110, and Aloo Bhujia 200 g. They are the planner mother’s basket and sell the most: about 12 a week each per store, about ₹5,700 a month each, and they leave about ₹45 a kilo. Regional heroes: Jolokia and Jhalmuri now, 40 Aana later. These are what national brands cannot copy quickly, an Assam and Bengal taste. Kids: Rings, 12 packs of ₹5 with water toys, and Noodles, 6 packs of ₹10, at the child’s eye level. They earn the most per kilo, ₹82 and ₹62, because extruded snacks use cheap inputs. Bulk: a new 1 kg family pack at ₹249. It answers the per gram shopper and raises the bill size without cutting the 400 g price. A party box of 12 follows later. Premium: a 500 g gift tin at ₹199 for Bihu, Puja and Diwali. Per store, the plan is about 386 packs and 116 kilos a month, about ₹32,000 at MRP, which leaves about ₹48 a kilo. That is about 8% of a Vishal Assam store’s snack sales, using JM Financial’s FMCG figure and my 8% snack share assumption. Shelf life is 4 to 9 months, and we deliver with at least two thirds left. Top right: per gram, Haldiram’s 400 g is ₹0.25. Our 400 g is 10% dearer and Vishal’s own label 7% dearer. The 1 kg pack matches Haldiram’s, so we answer price with a pack, not a price cut. Bottom right, the raw material plan for the modern trade range alone: about 17 tonnes of gram flour, 19 of palm oil and 17 of fresh potato in FY29, about three times that by FY31, bought at harvest and on contracts.” Engagement: “Which of these are in production today, and is a 1 kg pouch possible on our current lines?” Transition: “Now, where do we put them first?”
