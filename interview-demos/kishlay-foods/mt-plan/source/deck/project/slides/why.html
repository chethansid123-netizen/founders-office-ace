WHERE WE STAND (75 seconds). “Three facts about today. Kishlay Foods did ₹288 crore in FY25, down 16%, with a net loss of ₹6.31 crore; those are the MCA filings via Tracxn. The range is wide, 236 products on the website, and most volume is ₹5 and ₹10 packs through kirana. My working assumption is that only about 2% of sales come through modern trade and e-commerce; that is the first number I want to replace with real data. The middle chart is the core insight, and it is a model on public prices with my cost assumptions, so I treat it as a hypothesis. Take one kilo of bhujia. In ₹10 pouches through kirana it leaves about ₹13 after GST, trade margins, raw material, packing, conversion, freight and schemes. Put the same kilo in a 400-gram pack and it leaves about ₹45 to ₹46, whether in kirana or a modern-trade store, and about ₹51 on quick commerce. So the pack, not the channel, makes the money. Modern trade and quick commerce are simply where 200-gram, 400-gram and multipacks sell, because those shoppers stock up. Kids’ multipacks of extruded rings leave about ₹82 a kilo. Pricing trap to avoid: per gram we are about 10% dearer than Haldiram’s 400-gram pack. If we cut our 400 g to ₹99 to match, we keep only 60% of the money per kilo. A 1 kg pack at ₹249 matches Haldiram’s per gram and still leaves ₹30 a pack. On the right, the market is moving our way: FMCG grew 7.8% in Oct–Dec 2025, modern trade’s growth tripled, e-commerce is 14% of FMCG in metros and quick commerce is three quarters of it. Peers show the pace: Bikaji gets about 3% from e-com and QC and doubles it yearly at 13.7% EBITDA; Prataap is at 1.2–1.5% on QC and aims for about 5%.” Engagement: “Do you know the gram weight of our ₹10 namkeen pouch and the plant cost per kilo? Those two numbers firm up this chart.” Transition: “But before we chase shelves, we fix the margin.”
