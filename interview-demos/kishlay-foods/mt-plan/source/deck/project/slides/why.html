WHERE WE STAND (75 seconds). “Three facts about today. Kishlay Foods did ₹288 crore in FY25, down 16%, with a net loss of ₹6.31 crore; those are the MCA filings via Tracxn. The range is wide, 236 products on the website, and most volume is ₹5 and ₹10 packs through kirana. My working assumption is that only about 2% of sales come through modern trade and online; that is the first number I want to replace with real data. The middle panel is the core insight. It is a model on public prices with my cost assumptions, so I treat it as a hypothesis. Take one kilo of bhujia in a 400 gram pack: it leaves about ₹46 in kirana, ₹45 in modern trade and ₹51 on quick commerce. About the same. Now change the pack: in ₹10 pouches the same kilo leaves only ₹13, while kids’ multipacks of rings leave ₹82. So the pack, not the channel, makes the money, and modern trade and quick commerce are where take home packs and multipacks sell, because those shoppers stock up. Pricing trap to avoid: per gram we are about 10% dearer than Haldiram’s 400 gram pack. If we cut our 400 g to ₹99 to match, we keep only 60% of the money per kilo. A 1 kg pack at ₹249 matches Haldiram’s per gram and still leaves ₹30 a pack. On the right, the market is moving our way: FMCG grew 7.8% in October to December 2025, modern trade’s growth tripled, ecommerce is 14% of FMCG in metros and quick commerce is three quarters of it. Peers show the pace: Bikaji gets about 3% from online and quick commerce and doubles it yearly at 13.7% EBITDA; Prataap is at 1.2 to 1.5% on quick commerce and aims for about 5%; Gopal runs at 6.7% EBITDA.” Engagement: “Do you know the gram weight of our ₹10 namkeen pouch and the plant cost per kilo? Those two numbers firm up this chart.” Transition: “But before we chase shelves, we fix the margin.”
