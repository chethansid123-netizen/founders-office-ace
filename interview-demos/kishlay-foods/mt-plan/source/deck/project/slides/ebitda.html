FIX EBITDA FIRST (90 seconds). “You asked me to keep EBITDA in mind before sales, so this comes before any store. Raw materials are 70 to 75% of cost, per CRISIL, so the purchase desk is the biggest lever. Left side, in basis points of revenue at full effect, each with an owner. One, buy gram flour at harvest, March to May, near the MSP of ₹5,875 a quintal, and hold 60 to 90 days of palm oil cover with Haldia or Kolkata refiners: 60 bps. Two, pay trade schemes on sell out, not on what we push into the distributor: 40. Three, cut the tail of 236 SKUs, which also cuts changeovers on the lines: 40. Four, thinner film, standard roll widths and a reverse auction among three converters: 30. Five, frying yield and oil uptake control in the plant: 30. Six, milk runs and full trucks: 25. Then a little from mix and from auditing modern trade claims against the purchase order and a store photo. Less 30 bps for the team, promoters and ads that modern trade needs. Net: 215 bps, from about 6% to about 8.2%. Packing Vishal’s own label namkeen would add about 40 more, but I have kept it out of Base. Right side: the levers ramp quarter by quarter. The margin crosses 7% in month 15, March 2028, and reaches about 8.15% by month 24. Slow takes 21 months, Fast 9. The big unknown is the start: I assumed 6%. If it is 5%, correction takes 24 months; if it is 4%, these levers alone are not enough and we would need own label packing or price mix. For reference, Gopal runs at 6.7% EBITDA and Bikaji at 13.7%. The gate: we test and prove modern trade in Guwahati and Assam meanwhile, but we do not expand beyond Assam until EBITDA holds 7% for two quarters.” Engagement: “What is our real EBITDA today, and how far ahead does purchase buy gram and oil?” Transition: “With the margin plan in place, who are we selling to on that shelf?”
