WHERE WE START (90 seconds). “Not all stores at once. We test in a few very good stores near the plant, then follow the proof outward. Read the road from left to right. Stop 1, January to March 2027: five strong stores in Guwahati, all within 30 km of the Amingaon plant, plus two control stores without our range. A city van delivers the same day, three times a week, at about ₹4 a kilo. Stop 2, FY28: Assam, from Nalbari at 60 km to Silchar at 320, 25 stores by March 2028 through town distributors, ₹3 to ₹12 a kilo. Stop 3, FY29 to FY30, only after EBITDA holds 7%: the rest of the Northeast and North Bengal, Shillong, Dimapur, Dibrugarh, Siliguri and Imphal, 74 then 120 stores, ₹5 to ₹19 a kilo. Stop 4, FY31: Kolkata. At 1,050 km a truck costs about ₹35 a kilo, so Kolkata needs a depot or a contract packer first. Panel A, which chain first: on a weighted score Vishal Mega Mart wins at 4.3 out of 5. It has 74 stores in the Northeast, 44 of them in Assam across 34 towns, and its Assam stores sell 31% more per square foot than its average, per JM Financial. Local supermarkets are fast to list for a pilot. DMart has no stores in the East today. Panel B, which stores: I weight footfall and FMCG density at 25% each, and 20% on the store manager. That comes from a Harvard Business School study of a retail chain: new products first placed with strong managers reached 28.5% more stores, and their odds of entering a store were 24% against 5%. So I would pick three Vishal stores, one Reliance Smart and one local supermarket. Panel C, supply and quality: city van, 17 foot truck to town distributors, refill two to three times a week, deliver with at least two thirds of shelf life left, 95% fill rate, and batch checks on moisture, frying oil and nitrogen flush.” Engagement: “Which vehicles do we use today for Guwahati deliveries, and what does a trip cost?” Transition: “Getting into those stores means winning one person: the category manager.”
