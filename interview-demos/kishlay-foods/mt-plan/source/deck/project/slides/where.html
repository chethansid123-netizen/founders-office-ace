WHERE FIRST (90 seconds). “Not all stores at once. We test in a few very good stores near the plant, then follow the proof outward. Phase 1, January to March 2027: five strong stores in Guwahati, all within 30 km of the Amingaon plant, plus two matched control stores without our range. A city van delivers the same day, three times a week, at about ₹4 a kilo. Phase 2, FY28: Assam, from Nalbari at 60 km to Silchar at 320, 25 stores by March 2028 through town distributors. Phase 3, FY29 to FY30, after EBITDA holds 7%: the rest of the Northeast and North Bengal, Shillong, Dimapur, Dibrugarh, Siliguri and Imphal, 74 then 120 stores, at ₹5 to ₹19 a kilo. Phase 4, FY31: Kolkata. At 1,050 km a truck costs about ₹35 a kilo, so Kolkata needs a depot or a co-packer first. Which chain first? On a weighted score Vishal Mega Mart wins: 74 Northeast stores, 44 of them in Assam across 34 towns, and its Assam stores sell 31% more per square foot than its average, per JM Financial. Local supermarkets are fast to list for a pilot. DMart has no stores in the East today. Which stores? I weight footfall and FMCG density, and 20% on the store manager. That comes from a Harvard Business School study of a retail chain: new products first placed with strong managers reached 28.5% more stores, and their odds of entering a store were 24% against 5%. So I would pick three Vishal stores, one Reliance Smart and one local supermarket. Supply and quality: city van, 17-foot truck to town distributors, refill two to three times a week, deliver with at least two-thirds of shelf life left, 95% fill rate, and batch checks on moisture, frying oil and nitrogen flush.” Engagement: “Which vehicles do we use today for Guwahati deliveries, and what does a trip cost?” Transition: “Getting into those stores means winning one person: the category manager.”
