WIN THE BUYER (90 seconds). “A category manager thinks in sales per store per week. So we go in with data, not discounts. Left: one store, one month, at pilot terms. About ₹32,400 of sales at MRP. GST takes ₹1,542, the retailer’s 25% takes about ₹8,100 and the distributor’s 4% about ₹1,300, so we receive about ₹21,450. Product cost, which is raw material, pack, conversion, freight and promotions, is about ₹16,500, leaving about ₹4,940 a month. The pilot spend is about ₹33,000 a store, mostly a weekend promoter and sampling for three months, so it pays back in about 6.7 months. To pay back in six, a store must sell about ₹36,000 a month: that is our bar. Middle: how she reads us. She ranks SKUs A, B and C by weekly sales; C gets delisted in 8 to 12 weeks. Our gate is anchors at 10 a week and others at 5, for two four-week cycles. If two of our five stores clear it, she calls us. That is when we negotiate for store six onwards: margin from 25% to 22% is worth about ₹970 a store a month, and credit from 60 to 45 days frees about ₹9,300 of cash per store. Across 74 stores, about ₹8.6 lakh a year plus ₹6.9 lakh of cash. We give weekly sell-out reports, promoters and sampling, a 95% fill rate with next-day refill from Amingaon and co-funded festive promotions; we ask for POS data, no listing fee, eye level, 45-day credit and festive end caps. Right: who pays when. DMart pays in about a week. Vishal’s payable days were 50 in FY25, and JM Financial notes about 90 days’ credit tied to roughly 80% sell-through. Reliance Smart and Spencer’s run 45 to 75 days. Every crore of modern-trade sales at 60 days ties up ₹16 lakh; by FY31 our MT and QC receivables are about ₹2 crore, costing about ₹22 lakh a year. So we push with margin, promoters, fill rate and the best-run stores, and pull with eye level, sampling, kids’ packs and app ads.” Engagement: “What credit terms do our current distributors give, and do we get any POS data today?” Transition: “Finally, quick commerce and the numbers.”
