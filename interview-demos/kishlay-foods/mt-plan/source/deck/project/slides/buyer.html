WIN THE BUYER (90 seconds). “A category manager thinks in sales per store per week. So we go in with data, not discounts. Panel 1, one store, one month, at pilot terms. About ₹32,400 of sales at MRP. GST takes ₹1,542, the retailer’s 25% about ₹8,100 and the distributor’s 4% about ₹1,300, so we receive about ₹21,450. Product cost, which is raw material, pack, conversion, freight and promotions, is about ₹16,500, so we keep about ₹4,940 a month. The pilot spend is about ₹33,000 a store, mostly a weekend promoter and sampling for three months, so it pays back in about 6.7 months. To pay back in six, a store must sell about ₹36,000 a month: that is our bar. Panel 2, how she reads us: she ranks every SKU A, B or C on weekly sales; A keeps its facings, B is watched, C goes in 8 to 12 weeks. Our own gate: anchors at 10 a week and other SKUs at 5, for two four week cycles in at least two of the five stores. Panel 3: when two of five clear it, she calls us. That is when we ask, for store six onwards: margin from 25% to 22%, worth about ₹970 a store a month, and credit from 60 to 45 days, which frees about ₹9,300 of cash per store. Across 74 stores, about ₹8.6 lakh a year plus ₹6.9 lakh of cash. Panel 4, who pays when: DMart pays in about 6 days, local stores in about 21 (my assumption), More in 30 to 60, Vishal’s payable days were 50 in FY25 with credit up to 90 days tied to 80% sell through, Reliance Smart and Spencer’s 45 to 75. Every crore of modern trade sales at 60 days ties up ₹16 lakh; by FY31 our receivables are about ₹2 crore, costing about ₹22 lakh a year. Panel 5, the trade we offer: weekly sell out data for store data, promoters and sampling for no listing fee and eye level, a 95% fill rate with next day refill for 45 day credit from store six, festive promotions shared 50:50 for festival end caps, and sale or return in the pilot for a fair eight week trial. We push with margin, promoters, fill rate and the best run stores, and pull with eye level, sampling, kids’ packs and app ads.” Engagement: “What credit terms do our current distributors give, and do we get any store sales data today?” Transition: “Finally, quick commerce and the numbers.”
