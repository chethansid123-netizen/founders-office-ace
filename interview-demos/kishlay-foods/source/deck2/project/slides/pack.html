PACK PHYSICS (60 s). “From first principles, three forces design the pack. Humidity: Assam and Bengal monsoons kill crunch, so ₹5 and ₹10 snacks go into nitrogen-flushed, metallised BOPP or polyester pouches — the nitrogen also cushions the product, which is why packets look half air. The coin: the pack has to hit exactly ₹5 or ₹10, so the grammage inside is what moves. Household use: family namkeen goes into a re-sealable 400 g zipper pouch at ₹110 so it stays fresh across several tea times. Then cartons for Choco Tik, corn flakes and the 180 g Nutri Zing box; single-serve sachets for pickles, imli chutney and ketchup; tetra packs and PET bottles for drinks; flow-wrap and boxes for muffins, slice cake and sweets.” Then the chart: shelf life sets distribution radius. Eggless muffins last about a month, rasgulla one to two, cookies and Gulab Jamun three to four, Crispy Rusk Toast five, namkeen and noodles six to nine, cheese bhujia and Assam tea twelve. “So short-life bakery must be made and sold close to the plant on tight beats, while long-life namkeen and tea are what you export to Bhutan or push to far states.”
