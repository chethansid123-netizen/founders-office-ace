01 · First principles

The whole business reduces to five equations.

The equation

Kishlay today

So

1 · Value per truck

₹ per truck = packs × MRP; a truck fills on volume.

Chips, puffs and rings are mostly air: low rupees per cubic metre.

Make near the eater: 3 plant sites inside the markets they serve.

2 · Unit margin

Profit = MRP − GST − trade − inputs − overheads.

₹10 MRP → ~₹7.22 reaches Kishlay → ~₹0.25 left (est.).

MRP is a coin, so grammage and cost are the only levers.

3 · Capacity

Output = installed capacity × utilisation.

~2,600 MT a month; roughly 50–75% used (est.).

Fixed cost is under-absorbed: sell more through the same lines.

4 · Reach

Sales = active outlets × sales per outlet.

2 lakh outlets × ~₹17k a year each (est.).

Productivity per outlet, not outlet count, is the lever.

5 · Working capital

Cash tied up = SKUs × days of stock × cost.

900+ SKUs; current ratio 0.95–1.06×; ~78–79% of bank limits used.

Fewer, faster SKUs free cash.

Sources: CRISIL Ratings (cost shares, liquidity); kishlayfoods.com; job posts (900+ SKUs) · est. = my estimate, working in the playbook

FIVE EQUATIONS (60 s). “Strip away the brands and the business is five equations. One, value per truck: a truck of chips fills up on volume long before weight, so freight per rupee of sales is high — which is why Kishlay makes in Guwahati, Hajipur and Chhattisgarh, inside the markets it sells to. Two, unit margin: from a ₹10 MRP, after 5% GST and trade margins, about ₹7.22 reaches Kishlay; inputs are 70–75% of cost, and what’s left is about 25 paise by my estimate. Because MRP is a coin, the only levers are grammage and cost. Three, capacity: about 2,600 tonnes a month, and my estimate is 50 to 75% used — so fixed cost is under-absorbed. Four, reach: two lakh outlets times roughly ₹17k a year each. Five, working capital: 900-plus SKUs, a current ratio around one, and bank limits about 78–79% used. Every slide that follows is one of these five terms.”
