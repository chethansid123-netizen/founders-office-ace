HUB-AND-SPOKE (60–75 s). Read it top to bottom, like the flow of goods. “Inputs: potato, wheat flour, corn, palm oil, gram flour, sugar and laminates — 70 to 75% of operating cost. Plants: five units in four locations. The own hub in North Guwahati — Brahmaputra Industrial Park, 2,00,000-plus sq ft, 2,000 MT — makes chips, rings, snacks, popcorn and namkeen, plus a dedicated instant-noodles plant from 2023, and serves the Northeast and East. Two third-party plants sit inside the other markets: AFP in Hajipur, Bihar, 300 MT a month, for Bihar and the north and east; GUD in Chhattisgarh, 300 MT a month, for central India; there are also external partners like Foodees Group. This is equation one — value per truck — in action: instead of trucking air across the Siliguri Corridor, they make close to the eater. Two companies: Tulsi Speciality Foods manufactures — 512 people, and it’s the ‘Manufactured by’ name on the pack — while Kishlay Foods owns the brands and sells, the ‘Marketed by’ name. CRISIL treats them as one group. Four ways out: general trade to two lakh outlets in 17+ states and 317 towns, BigBasket for e-commerce, exports to Bhutan, and contract manufacturing for Frito-Lay and Britannia — which fills capacity when own-brand demand is soft.” Question to ask: how is capacity split between own brands and contract manufacturing?
