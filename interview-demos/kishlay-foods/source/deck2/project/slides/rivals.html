COMPETITION (60–75 s). “Kishlay doesn’t have one competitor; it has a different one in every category on the same hanging strip. In potato chips it’s Lay’s, Bingo!, Balaji and Yellow Diamond, and Kishlay’s edge is local, fresher stock. In rings and extruded snacks it’s Kurkure, Yellow Diamond, DFM’s Crax and Too Yumm!, fought with toy-in-pack and grammage. In namkeen, Haldiram’s, Bikaji, Gopal and Annapurna Swadisht — Kishlay’s edge is eastern flavours like Jolokia and Jhalmuri and ₹5 sachets. In noodles, CG Foods’ Wai Wai dominates the Northeast, alongside Maggi and YiPPee!; Kishlay answers with a ₹5 pack and a chicken masala flavour. In biscuits and cakes it’s Britannia, Parle, Bisk Farm, Anmol and Mrs. Bectors. And locally, loose chanachur, bakeries and copycat packs — Kishlay has won trade-dress cases in the Gauhati High Court (2019) and the Delhi High Court (2026). BDO names Pran, Kishlay and Annapurna Swadisht as the three regional snack leaders of East India.” Five forces in one line each: rivalry very high; supplier power high because commodities are 70–75% of cost; buyer power high because coin MRPs are fixed and distributors want margin; substitutes like street food; new entrants easy locally, hard at Kishlay’s multi-state scale. Then the economics: same shelf, but Gopal earns 7.2% and Bikaji 10.5% at four to seven times the scale.
