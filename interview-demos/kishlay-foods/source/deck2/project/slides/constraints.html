12 · Constraints

The machine works. Four constraints decide
what it earns next.

1 · Inputs on a fixed coin

Commodities are 70–75% of cost; the FY22 spike in wheat, sugar and palm oil meant a ₹20.7 Cr loss.

Fix: a grammage playbook, forward buying, more ₹20+ mix.

2 · SKU complexity

236 listed, 292+ claimed, 900+ with sizes: about ₹38 lakh of sales per SKU a year (est.).

Fix: an SKU cube (velocity × margin), cut the tail, 25–30 must-stock heroes.

3 · Capacity and freight

Roughly 50–75% of 2,600 MT a month used (est.); three plants with overlapping ranges.

Fix: load the hub first; make each SKU at its nearest plant; track freight ₹/kg by lane.

4 · Cash and reporting

Current ratio 0.95–1.06×, ~78–79% of bank limits used; CRISIL flagged missing monthly data (June 2026).

Fix: a weekly MIS and distributor secondary-sales data.

What I’d own: one weekly plant-to-outlet MIS that tracks the five equations — margin per pack, utilisation, sales per outlet, SKU velocity and cash.

Sources: CRISIL Ratings (costs, FY22, liquidity, June 2026); job posts · est. = ₹344 Cr ÷ 900 SKUs

CONSTRAINTS AND CLOSE (60–75 s). “The machine works — it has run for 23 years and reaches two lakh shelves. Four constraints decide what it earns next. One, inputs on a fixed coin: commodities are 70–75% of cost, and when wheat, sugar and palm oil spiked in FY22 the group lost ₹20.7 Cr. The fix is a grammage playbook written before the next spike, forward buying where cash allows, and more ₹20-plus mix. Two, SKU complexity: 236 products listed, 292-plus claimed, 900-plus counting sizes — about ₹38 lakh of sales per SKU a year by my maths. Build an SKU cube, velocity times margin, cut the tail, and make 25–30 heroes must-stock everywhere. Three, capacity and freight: if only 50 to 75% of capacity is used, load the hub first, make each SKU at its nearest plant, and track freight per kilo by lane. Four, cash and reporting: a current ratio around one, bank limits about 78–79% used, and CRISIL noted in June 2026 that monthly information wasn’t submitted — so a weekly MIS and distributor secondary-sales data are overdue.” Close: “What I’d own is one weekly plant-to-outlet MIS that tracks the five equations from slide 2: margin per pack, utilisation, sales per outlet, SKU velocity and cash.” Then ask: “Which of these four constraints keeps you up at night?”
