PRICE-PACK ARCHITECTURE (60–75 s). “Every product sits on one of four coin tiers. Tier 1, ₹5, the mass impulse core: chips, rings with toys, fryums, sticks, even a 60 g eggless muffin, a 12.5 g Gold Tea sachet, noodles and Choco Tik. Tier 2, ₹10: Cocktail Peanut, Zing chips and noodles, Slice Fruit Cake, 25 g Gold Tea, soya chunks. Tier 3, ₹20 to ₹55, mid-size and sharing: Potato Feast at ₹20, Crispy Rusk Toast ₹30, Dry Rusk ₹40, cookies ₹35–55, 200 g namkeen ₹55. Tier 4, ₹110 to ₹250, family and gifting: 400 g zipper bhujia at ₹110, the 180 g Nutri Zing box, Gulab Jamun ₹230, Rasgulla ₹250.” First principles: in tiers 1 and 2 the price is a coin and cannot move, so weight is the variable — packs carry ‘25% extra’ when costs ease, and grammage drops quietly when palm oil or potato spikes. The CEO has said the ₹5 and ₹10 points drive the bulk of sales, which is why tier 3 and 4 mix matters for margin. Why 236 products become 900-plus SKUs: each product multiplies by price point and pack size — ₹5, ₹10, ₹20, 50, 110, 180, 200, 400 g and cartons. That multiplication is the working-capital equation from slide 2.
