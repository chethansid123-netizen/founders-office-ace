DISTRIBUTION (60–75 s). “Goods move through three tiers of trade. From the factory depot to super-stockists or C&F agents, who buy in container loads on credit; to district and town distributors, who run the beats and vans; to wholesalers like Fancy Bazar in Guwahati; and to two lakh-plus retailers — kiranas, neighbourhood bakeries, tea stalls and highway kiosks. Each plant feeds its own lanes: Guwahati the Northeast, the East and Bhutan; Hajipur Bihar and the north and east; Chhattisgarh central India. Short lanes are equation one again — fresh ₹5 packs and low freight per rupee. The field force, from Tulsi’s job postings: a VP Sales at ₹30–45 lakh, an RSM for the North East at ₹15–20 lakh, ASMs at ₹7–9 lakh including a dedicated noodles ASM in Tripura, and sales officers in Guwahati, Silchar, Dibrugarh, Tezpur, Nagaon and Tinsukia. Beyond general trade: BigBasket for 110–400 g packs, B2B marketplaces, exports to Bhutan, and co-packing for Frito-Lay and Britannia.” The numbers: about 630 outlets per town, about ₹17k sales per outlet a year, and by my estimate the trade keeps about ₹2.30 of every ₹10 — retailer about ₹1.60 and stockist plus distributor about 70 paise. The lever is sales per outlet: more lines per call (must-stock SKUs) and bigger drops (₹20–110 packs), on the beats that already exist.
