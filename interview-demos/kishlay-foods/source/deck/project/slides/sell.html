06 / 09

SHELF + SHOPPER (60–75 s). “Downstream, the decision is tiny and fast. Two people matter. The customer is the retailer — he decides what hangs on that strip, based on margin, credit and how fast it turns. The consumer then decides in about three seconds: a hunger cue, a set of three or four brands they already know, a shelf filter — is it there, is it ₹5 or ₹10, does it look fresh — and they grab on colour, mascot or a free toy. Taste decides repeat.” Insight from the court record: in Kishlay v. Ridhi Sidhi (Gauhati High Court, 2019) the court protected Non Stop noodles’ trade dress because village buyers recognise packs by overall look, not fine print. So pack design is the brand. Three shoppers: the kid buys the toy (Water Toy Rings, Balloon Rings, Khaao Khelo) and drives pester power; the spice-seeker (Flames, Super Max Hot, Jolokia Chanachur) switches every week, so Kishlay keeps that switching inside its own range with dozens of shapes; the chai homemaker buys Mamooz, Bisk Baker rusk, 400 g bhujia and Gold Tea as a ritual and is loyal if she trusts quality. Consumer insight: Justdial rates Kishlay 4.1/5 over 92 reviews, but one review of Hot ’n’ Spicy says the only taste is chilli powder — Eastern palates want heat with masala depth (cumin, black salt, amchur), not just chilli.
