FIRST PRINCIPLES (60–75 s). “Before Kishlay, ask why a regional snack company should exist at all. Four physical facts. One: a chips or puff packet is mostly air — a truck fills up on volume long before its weight limit, so freight per rupee of sales is brutal. Two: the whole Northeast hangs off India through the Siliguri Corridor, about 22 km wide, so everything from a mainland plant takes a long, single-lane route. Three: Assam and Bengal are humid; old stock goes soggy, so freshness is taste. Four: the shopper pays in coins — ₹5 and ₹10 — so you can’t pass freight on. Put those together and the answer is: make it where it’s eaten. That is Kishlay’s founding advantage: a 2,000-tonne hub in North Guwahati, plus third-party plants in Hajipur and Chhattisgarh for Bihar, Bengal and central India.” Then the need gap in one line: today’s small-town shopper gets loose chanachur or stale national packs; Kishlay bridges to fresh, hygienic, local-flavoured snacks for a coin. Six needs it serves: convenience, affordability, bold taste, cultural familiarity (Jolokia, Poochka, Jhalmuri), sharing (family packs), variety.
