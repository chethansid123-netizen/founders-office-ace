DISTRIBUTION + THE ₹10 WATERFALL (75–90 s). This slide pays off the hook. “Here’s how a ₹10 packet travels: plant, C&F depot, super-stockist who buys in bulk on credit, distributor who runs the beats and vans, the kirana, the shopper. In the Northeast it runs through a VP Sales, a Regional Sales Manager for the North East, Area Sales Managers — including a dedicated one for noodles in Tripura — and sales officers in Guwahati, Silchar, Dibrugarh, Tezpur, Nagaon and Tinsukia. Now follow the money. Out of ₹10, about 48 paise is GST at the new 5% rate, about ₹1.60 is the retailer’s margin, about 70 paise goes to the stockist and distributor. Roughly ₹7.20 reaches Kishlay. About 70% of that is raw material and packaging. Overheads — conversion, freight, people, ads — take most of the rest. What’s left, at CRISIL’s 2.5–4% range, is about 25 paise.” Then the two productivity numbers: ₹344 Cr over 2 lakh outlets is about ₹17,200 per outlet per year — roughly six ₹10 packs a day per shop (in MRP terms). And the sensitivity: a 1% rise in input costs is ~5 paise a pack, a fifth of the margin — which is why the industry changes grammage, not MRP. Be explicit: “the trade margins are my assumptions; the cost shares are from CRISIL.” Upper-bound caveat: revenue includes contract manufacturing and exports, so true GT productivity per outlet is a bit lower.
