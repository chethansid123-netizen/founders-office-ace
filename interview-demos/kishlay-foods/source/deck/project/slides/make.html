MANUFACTURING (60–75 s). “Upstream, this is a commodity converter: potatoes, wheat flour, palm oil, corn, gram flour, sugar and laminates are 70–75% of operating cost, per CRISIL. They run a hub-and-spoke footprint: the own North Guwahati hub — 2,00,000+ sq ft, 2,000 MT — with four units inside: potato-chip lines (Siddhivinayak PLC lines, 200–1,500 kg/hr), Fabcon extruders for rings and pasta shapes, balls and puffs, and biscuits; plus a 2023 instant-noodles plant. Then third-party plants at Hajipur (AFP, 300 MT/month) for Bihar and the north, and Chhattisgarh (GUD, 300 MT/month) for central India. Legally, Tulsi Speciality Foods manufactures — 512 people — and Kishlay Foods owns the brands and markets.” The capacity check: “If I divide FY24 sales by a reasonable ₹150–220 per kg net realisation, they sold roughly 15–23 thousand tonnes against ~31 thousand tonnes of capacity — about 50–75% used. If that’s right, the bottleneck isn’t the factory; it’s demand pull. I’d love to know the real utilisation.” Quality: ISO 22000:2018, HACCP, FSSAI; six-stage QA. Shelf life drives logistics: muffins ~1 month, rasgulla 1–2, cookies 3–4, rusk 5, namkeen and noodles 6–9, tea and cheese bhujia 12 months.
