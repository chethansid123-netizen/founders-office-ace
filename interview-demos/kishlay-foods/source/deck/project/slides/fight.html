07 / 09

COMPETITION (60 s). “Kishlay fights on three fronts. Nationals — PepsiCo’s Lay’s and Kurkure, ITC’s Bingo! and YiPPee!, Britannia, Parle, Haldiram’s, Bikaji, Nestlé’s Maggi — win on ad budgets and distributor clout; interestingly, Frito-Lay and Britannia are also Kishlay’s contract-manufacturing clients. The fiercest fight is with East and Northeast regionals playing the same ₹5 game: Pran, Annapurna Swadisht, Bisk Farm, Anmol, CG Foods’ Wai Wai in noodles, Prataap’s Yellow Diamond and DFM’s Crax in rings with toys. And locally, loose chanachur, bakeries, street food and copycats — Kishlay won against ‘Motu Rama’ noodles in the Gauhati High Court in 2019 and in 2026 got Mrs. Bectors to agree to change the trade dress of 11 ‘NON STOP’ biscuits in the Delhi High Court.” Then the table: “Look at the economics. Gopal is about four times Kishlay’s revenue at 7.2% EBITDA; Bikaji seven times at 10.5%. Gopal reaches about 5 lakh outlets and sells about ₹29k per outlet a year versus Kishlay’s ~₹17k. So the gap isn’t the factory — it’s pull: brand, mix and outlet productivity.” Porter in one line: rivalry very high, buyer power high (fixed coin MRPs, trade margins), substitutes high (street food), supplier power medium-high (commodities 70–75% of cost), entry moderate (easy locally, hard at Kishlay’s multi-state scale).
