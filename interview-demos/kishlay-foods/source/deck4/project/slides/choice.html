THE STRATEGIC CHOICE (60–75 s). “If the ₹5 shelf is crowded, the next win may not be more products — it may be a sharper portfolio and a stronger regional edge. Ansoff puts growth in order. Priority one, market penetration: sell more where Kishlay already is — make sure the hero SKUs are always on the shelf and raise productivity per outlet. Lowest risk, fastest payback. Priority two, product development: test regional premium packs — Jolokia, Jhalmuri, 40 Aana in larger formats — but only where the proposition is defensible. Priority three, market development: expand into new states and channels only after demand and unit economics are proven. Diversification, not now — 18 categories is already a lot. Blue Ocean’s ERRC grid says the same thing in four moves: eliminate look-alike SKUs like Onion Ringo versus Onion Rin-Goo; reduce new ₹5 shapes that add complexity; raise hero availability; create regional premium packs. And I want to be honest about what must be validated first: which 25–30 SKUs really drive sales, whether shoppers will pay ₹55–110 for regional flavours, and whether modern trade and quick commerce leave a margin after listing fees and platform costs. Regional premium is a hypothesis, not a proven gap.” Transition: “How do we turn this choice into commercial execution?”
