THE STRATEGIC CHOICE (75 s). “This is a Blue Ocean strategy canvas. Across the bottom are the seven things a ₹5 shopper and a kirana weigh up: low price, range breadth, ad spend, made locally, regional taste, premium packs and shelf reach. The heights are my judgement, not measured data. Watch the curves arrive. National brands, in blue, spend heavily on ads and reach. Regional value players, in grey, are all about low price. Now Kishlay today, in red: it sits almost on top of the regional value curve — low price, a very wide range, little advertising. That overlap is a red ocean: if you look like every other ₹5 rival, you can only compete on price. The dashed gold line is where I’d take it, using the ERRC moves underneath. Eliminate look-alike SKUs and reduce new ₹5 shapes, so range breadth comes down. Raise hero availability and regional taste. Create regional premium packs — Jolokia, Jhalmuri, 40 Aana in larger formats. On the right, Ansoff puts the growth in order: first penetration, selling more where Kishlay already is; second, testing regional premium products; third, new markets only once demand and unit economics are proven; and no new categories for now — there are already 18. Before any of this, three things must be validated: which 25 to 30 SKUs really drive sales, whether shoppers will pay ₹55–110 for regional flavours, and whether modern trade and quick commerce leave a margin. Regional premium is a hypothesis, not a proven gap.” Transition: “How do we turn this choice into commercial execution?”
