GO-TO-MARKET EXECUTION (75–90 s). “Strategy only matters if it becomes a pilot with a number attached. Each pilot follows the same six steps: need gap, 5C analysis — company, customer, competitors, collaborators, context — segmentation, targeting and positioning, the seven Ps, the pilot design, and the KPIs. Three pilots, one per channel. General trade: the hypothesis is that keeping 25–30 hero SKUs always in stock lifts sales per outlet. Run it in two or three Guwahati beats against control beats, with a must-stock list and weekly refills, owned by the Head of Sales for the Northeast over eight weeks. Measure lines per call, sales per outlet and stock-outs; scale if the pilot beats beat the control beats. Modern trade: the hypothesis is that regional 200–400 g packs — Jolokia, Jhalmuri, 400 g bhujia, cookies — earn their shelf at ₹55–110. Test in selected Guwahati stores with eye-level placement and sampling; stop if listing and promo costs eat the margin. Quick commerce: the hypothesis is that urban 18–35 buyers want multipacks delivered fast — Blinkit has served Guwahati since January 2025. Test four to six SKUs; scale only if repeat purchase is healthy and contribution after platform fees is positive. I haven’t put rupee costs on these — they need to be sized with finance — but I’ve named what has to be costed for each. Exports to Bhutan and Nepal are a separate feasibility study, not a pilot.” Transition: “This is how the Founder’s Office turns strategy into measurable outcomes.”
