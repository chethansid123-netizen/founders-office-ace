GO-TO-MARKET EXECUTION (75–90 s). “Strategy only matters if it becomes a pilot with a number attached. Every pilot follows the same six steps: need gap, 5C — company, customer, competitors, collaborators, context — segmentation, targeting and positioning, the seven Ps, the pilot design, and the KPIs. The timeline shows three pilots over 12 weeks, each ending in a decision point: scale, modify or stop. General trade, owned by the Head of Sales for the Northeast, runs eight weeks: the hypothesis is that keeping 25–30 hero SKUs always in stock lifts sales per outlet, tested in two or three Guwahati beats against control beats with a must-stock list and weekly refills. We measure lines per call, sales per outlet and stock-outs, and scale only if the test beats beat the control. Modern trade runs 8–12 weeks in selected Guwahati stores: regional 200–400 g packs — Jolokia, Jhalmuri, 400 g bhujia — at ₹55–110, at eye level with sampling; stop if listing and promo costs eat the margin. Quick commerce starts in week five, once general trade is running, and is owned by the Founder’s Office: four to six SKUs and multipacks for urban 18–35 buyers — Blinkit has served Guwahati since January 2025; scale only if repeat purchase is healthy and contribution after platform fees is positive. I haven’t put rupee costs on these — they need to be sized with finance — but each card names what has to be costed. Exports to Bhutan and Nepal are a separate feasibility study, not a pilot.” Transition: “This is how the Founder’s Office turns strategy into measurable outcomes.”
