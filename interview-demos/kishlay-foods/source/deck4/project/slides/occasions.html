PRODUCT AND CONSUMER STRATEGY (60–75 s). “The same shopper buys for different moments, and Kishlay has a pack and price for each — these occasion mappings are my hypotheses. Impulse: a cheap treat right now, often for the toy — rings, puffs and fryums in a ₹5 pouch. On the go: fill hunger fast — noodles, peanuts and Zing chips at ₹5 and ₹10. Evening chai: a savoury bite with tea — namkeen and bhujia from a ₹10 sachet to a ₹55 200 g pack. Family sharing: value for the household — the 400 g zipper pouch at ₹110 and Potato Feast at ₹20. Breakfast and tea: a trusted daily ritual — cookies, rusk, Choco Tik and tea from ₹5 to ₹55. Regional taste: the flavour of home — Jolokia, Jhalmuri and 40 Aana Chanachur. On the right, the 236 products on the website by family: 145 savoury snacks, 45 namkeen and noodles, 22 bakery, 19 pantry, 5 beverages. Breadth helps — one supplier fills many shelf slots for a distributor. But breadth costs: 236 products listed, 292-plus SKUs claimed on the About page, and 900-plus once every pack size is counted in job posts. These three numbers use different definitions, so I treat them as a range to validate, not one fact.” Transition: “But serving many occasions and price points creates a manufacturing challenge.”
