DISTRIBUTION AND UNIT ECONOMICS (75 s). “From the plant, a pack takes five hops: C&F agents and super-stockists buying in bulk on credit, distributors running town beats with vans, wholesalers across 317 towns, two lakh-plus retailers, and finally the consumer paying ₹5 or ₹10. Follow ₹10: about 48 paise is GST — that’s the actual 5% rate since September 2025 — about ₹1.60 the retailer’s margin and about 70 paise the stockist and distributor. Those trade cuts are industry-typical estimates, not Kishlay’s numbers. So about ₹7.22 comes back. Five numbers often get mixed up, so let me separate them. Revenue: ₹344 Cr in FY24, per CRISIL. Realisation: about ₹7.22 per ₹10 pack, my calculation. Gross margin isn’t disclosed; CRISIL only says raw materials are 70–75% of operating cost. Contribution margin — after freight, schemes and trade spend — isn’t public; that’s the first number I’d want internally. Net profit: CRISIL shows −3.4% in FY23 and −6.5% in FY22 — historical, and after those years it isn’t published. Reach versus productivity: two lakh-plus outlets reached, but not all are active. Revenue divided by outlets gives about ₹17k a year at factory price — about ₹24k at MRP, or roughly 6.5 ten-rupee packs a day — and that’s an upper bound because revenue includes co-packing and exports. I’m not putting a peer next to it, because no competitor publishes a like-for-like figure.” Transition: “Every shelf slot is contested — which brings us to competition.”
