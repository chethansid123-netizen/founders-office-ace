THE BUSINESS MODEL (60 s). “Kishlay’s answer to that problem is a simple model: make it close to the consumer, then sell it through many channels. Left to right: inputs — wheat, sugar, potato, corn and palm oil — go into three sites with five manufacturing units, get packed at price points from ₹5 to ₹250, and leave through general trade, online and export to two lakh-plus shops in 17-plus states. The picture shows the structure. The own hub at North Guwahati is listed at 2,000 MT; partner plants at Hajipur in Bihar and in Chhattisgarh add 300 MT a month each, so most capacity is owned. Tulsi Speciality Foods makes — it started as a partnership in 2017, began production in August 2018 and became a private limited company on 7 October 2024, with Sandeep and Vishesh Bajaj as directors — and Kishlay Foods owns the brands and sells. Why the model works: plants inside their markets mean less freight per rupee of sales and fresher stock, and spare lines can earn through co-packing for Frito-Lay and Britannia.” Data note: the website states Guwahati as “2,000 MT” without a period; I read it as monthly.
