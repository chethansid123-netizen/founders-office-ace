THE OPERATING ENGINE (60–75 s). “Serving many occasions lands on the factory. Kishlay owns the North Guwahati hub and uses partner plants at Hajipur and Chhattisgarh at 300 MT a month each. The hub is listed as 2,000 MT — the site doesn’t state a period, and I read it as monthly, which gives 2,600 MT a month in total. That is installed capacity, not output. Actual production isn’t published. My estimate: FY24 revenue of ₹344 Cr divided by a realisation of ₹150–220 a kilo gives 15,600 to 22,900 tonnes a year, or about 1,300 to 1,900 a month — roughly 50 to 75% of capacity. Two caveats: the revenue is FY24 while the capacity is today’s, and revenue includes co-packing. Why it matters, in one line: at half use, every kilo carries twice the fixed cost it would at full use. What limits it: four different lines — chips, extruders, puffs and biscuits — so spare chip capacity can’t make biscuits; a wide product mix means more changeovers; shelf life from one to twelve months means short-life bakery has to sell near the plant; and people — 512 staff at Tulsi, with absence peaking in July, the monsoon snacking season. Quality runs through six checks under ISO 22000, FSSAI and HACCP. So capacity alone doesn’t guarantee growth — demand does.” Transition: “Even once it’s made, the product has to be delivered — profitably.”
