THE CONSUMER PROBLEM (60 s). “Before the company, the consumer. Picture a ₹5 coin, a hungry moment and a kirana counter — that is the entire purchase. I believe the shopper wants six things — and I want to be clear these are hypotheses from how the category works, not survey results. One, affordability: a treat for ₹5 or ₹10. But the price is a coin, and raw materials are 70 to 75% of cost per CRISIL, so the maker absorbs every cost shock. Two, freshness and crunch: but monsoon humidity kills crunch, and shelf life ranges from about a month for muffins to a year for tea. Three, familiar regional taste — Jolokia, jhalmuri, chanachur — while national brands design for a national palate. Four, availability in the lane shop every day — but the whole Northeast joins India through a corridor about 22 km wide, and a truck of snacks fills on volume, not weight, so freight per rupee is high. Five, convenience — single-serve packs — which means more packing, more SKUs and more stock. Six, price sensitivity — shoppers switch for a better deal, so when costs rise only the grams can change. The gap between what the consumer wants and what is hard to deliver is the reason a business like Kishlay exists.” Transition: “So how has Kishlay built a system to solve this?”
