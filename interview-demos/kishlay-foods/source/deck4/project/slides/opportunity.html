THE OPPORTUNITY (45 s). “When someone buys a ₹5 snack, they make a small purchase decision. Behind that decision is a much larger system — sourcing, manufacturing, packaging, distribution and retailer economics. Kishlay runs that system at real scale: about ₹344 Cr of revenue in FY24 per CRISIL, a stated 2,600 tonnes a month of capacity across five units, two lakh-plus outlets in 17-plus states and 317 towns, and 236 products in 18 categories on its own website. One central question runs through this deck: how can Kishlay turn its manufacturing and distribution capabilities into more profitable, consumer-led growth? Let me start where the business starts — with the person buying the packet.” Note the labels under each number: Company means the company reports it; External means a third party such as CRISIL; Historical means it is from a past period; Calculation means I derived it; Hypothesis means it needs testing. Data notes: revenue is FY24 (latest public figure); capacity is installed capacity, not output, and the website lists the Guwahati unit as “2,000 MT” without a period — I have assumed monthly, consistent with the 300 MT/month partner plants; to validate.
