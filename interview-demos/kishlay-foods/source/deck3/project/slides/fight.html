FIGHT (75–90 s). Left to right. “Map the market by price focus and reach. The top-left is crowded: Kishlay, Pran, Annapurna Swadisht, Wai Wai, Bisk Farm, Balaji, Gopal and loose local namkeen all fight for the ₹5 hanging strip. The nationals — PepsiCo, ITC, Britannia, Parle, Haldiram’s, Bikaji, Yellow Diamond — sit lower, winning on reach and ad money. The top-right, regional premium, is nearly empty — and Kishlay’s range already stretches to ₹250. Five forces: rivalry 4.5 out of 5 with 130-plus tracked rivals; suppliers 4 — inputs are 70–75% of cost and in FY25 palm oil rose about 54% and potato about 56%; buyers 4 — the coin MRP can’t rise and distributors want margin and credit; substitutes 3.5 — street jhalmuri and samosa; new entrants 3 — easy locally, hard at two lakh outlets. Four of five are high: a red ocean. And on the same shelf, peers earn more: Gopal ₹1,468 Cr at 7.2% EBITDA, Bikaji ₹2,562 Cr at 10.5%, Kishlay ₹344 Cr at 2.5–4% per CRISIL; Gopal sells about ₹29k per outlet a year against Kishlay’s ₹17k. By aisle: Lay’s, Bingo! and Balaji in chips; Kurkure, Yellow Diamond and Crax in rings; Haldiram’s, Bikaji and Gopal in namkeen; Wai Wai, Maggi and YiPPee! in noodles; Britannia, Parle and Bisk Farm in biscuits; Pran, Annapurna and Anmol regionally. The gap is pull, not the factory.” Map positions and force scores are an analyst view; the drivers are sourced.
