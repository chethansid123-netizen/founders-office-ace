FIVE FORCES WITH THE NUMBERS (75–90 s). Say this first: “No one publishes five-forces scores, so I’ve rated each force on one rule — High means it is already cutting the margin, with numbers to show it.” Suppliers, High: Kishlay buys potato from farmers and cold stores, palm oil from refiners, wheat flour and sugar from mills, and pouches from laminate makers. These inputs are 70–75% of cost per CRISIL. In FY25 palm oil rose about 54% and potato about 56%, and Gopal’s gross margin fell from 28.1% to 20.2% in Q4 FY25. Kishlay itself lost ₹20.7 Cr in FY22 when wheat, sugar and palm oil spiked. Market prices set the cost and the coin MRP can’t pass it on. Buyers, High: super-stockists and distributors, two lakh-plus retailers, and shoppers paying in coins. The retailer keeps about ₹1.60 and the trade about 70 paise of every ₹10, MRP is fixed at ₹5 and ₹10, and Kishlay sells about ₹17k a year per outlet against Gopal’s ₹29k — so a shop can swap Kishlay out. The GST cut from 12% to 5% in September 2025 goes to shoppers as more grams, not to the maker. Rivalry, High: Tracxn tracks 130-plus competitors and Ken Research counts about 2,500 players in savoury snacks; Gopal is four times and Bikaji seven times Kishlay’s revenue and earn 7.2% and 10.5% EBITDA against 2.5–4%; and Kishlay’s growth slowed from 32% in FY20 to 2.5% in FY24. New entrants, Medium: easy at town level with a fryer and a pouch machine, hard at 2,600 MT a month and two lakh outlets; quick commerce — Blinkit in Guwahati since January 2025 — is a new door that skips distributors. Substitutes, Medium-high: loose chanachur, bakeries, jhalmuri and samosa, and copycats, which Kishlay has fought in the Gauhati High Court in 2019 and the Delhi High Court in 2026.
