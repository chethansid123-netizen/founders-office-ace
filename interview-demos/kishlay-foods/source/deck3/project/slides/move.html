MOVE (60–75 s). “Five hops: five plants dispatch by lane; C&F agents and super-stockists buy in bulk on credit; distributors run town beats with vans; wholesale markets like Fancy Bazar in 317 towns; and two lakh-plus kiranas, bakeries, tea stalls and kiosks. Follow ₹10: about 48 paise is GST at the new 5% rate, about ₹1.60 the retailer’s margin and about 70 paise the stockist and distributor — so about ₹7.22 comes back to Kishlay, and raw materials take 70 to 75% of that on a price that can’t rise. Reach versus depth: about 630 outlets per town, but each sells only about ₹17k a year — ₹344 Cr over two lakh outlets — against roughly ₹29k at Gopal. That’s about six ₹10 packs a shop a day. A 1% rise in input costs is about 5 paise a pack, which is why the industry moves grams, not MRP. Who runs it: a VP Sales at ₹30–45 lakh, an RSM for the North East at ₹15–20 lakh, ASMs at ₹7–9 lakh including a noodles ASM in Tripura, and sales officers in Guwahati, Silchar, Dibrugarh, Tezpur, Nagaon and Tinsukia. Lanes: Guwahati serves the Northeast, East and Bhutan; Hajipur Bihar, north and east; Chhattisgarh central India. So reach is won; sales per outlet is the lever.” Trade margins are industry-typical; revenue includes co-packing and exports, so per-outlet sales are an upper bound.
