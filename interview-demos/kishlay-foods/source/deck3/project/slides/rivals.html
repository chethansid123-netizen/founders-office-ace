WHO SELLS WHAT (75 s). Read left to right, tier by tier. “Kishlay fights 17 rivals in four tiers. International: PepsiCo with Lay’s and Kurkure, winning on ads and national reach; Nestlé with Maggi, which owns instant noodles; Pran from Bangladesh, cheap across chips, rings and juice; and CG Foods from Nepal with Wai Wai, the Northeast’s noodle habit. National snack makers: ITC’s Bingo! and YiPPee!; Haldiram’s in bhujia, sweets and premium family packs; Bikaji, ₹2,562 Cr at 10.5% EBITDA; Gopal, ₹1,468 Cr at 7.2%; and Balaji in value chips and namkeen in the west. Value and biscuits: Prataap’s Yellow Diamond and DFM’s Crax fight the ₹5 rings war; Britannia and Parle own mass biscuits. East and Northeast: Annapurna Swadisht, Bisk Farm and Anmol from Kolkata, Mrs. Bectors — which agreed in the Delhi High Court in 2026 to change the trade dress of 11 ‘NON STOP’ biscuits — and loose local chanachur. Head-to-head by product: Zing chips at ₹5 and ₹10 meet Lay’s, Bingo! and Balaji; ₹5 rings with a toy meet Yellow Diamond, Crax and Pran; 400 g namkeen at ₹110 meets Haldiram’s, Bikaji and Gopal; ₹5 and ₹10 noodles meet Wai Wai, Maggi and YiPPee!; ₹35–55 cookies meet Britannia, Parle and Bisk Farm. Everyone pays the same trade cut in general trade — about ₹2.30 of every ₹10 — but Gopal keeps 7.2% and Bikaji 10.5% EBITDA, against Kishlay’s 2.5–4% per CRISIL.” Margins are shown only where published; PepsiCo, ITC, Haldiram’s and the regionals don’t publish snack-level margins.
