WHO SELLS WHAT (60–75 s). Read left to right, tier by tier. “Kishlay fights 17 rivals in four tiers. International: PepsiCo with Lay’s and Kurkure; Nestlé with Maggi; Pran from Bangladesh across chips, rings and juice; and CG Foods from Nepal with Wai Wai, the Northeast’s noodle habit. National snack makers: ITC’s Bingo! and YiPPee!; Haldiram’s in bhujia and sweets; Bikaji, ₹2,562 Cr at 10.5% EBITDA; Gopal, ₹1,468 Cr at 7.2%; and Balaji in value chips and namkeen. Value and biscuits: Prataap’s Yellow Diamond and DFM’s Crax fight the ₹5 rings war; Britannia and Parle own mass biscuits. East and Northeast: Annapurna Swadisht, Bisk Farm and Anmol from Kolkata; Mrs. Bectors, which agreed in the Delhi High Court in 2026 to change the trade dress of 11 ‘NON STOP’ look-alike biscuits; and loose local chanachur. Along the bottom, each Kishlay pack and its main rival: Zing chips at ₹5 and ₹10 against Lay’s, Bingo! and Balaji; ₹5 rings with a toy against Yellow Diamond, Crax and Pran; 400 g bhujia at ₹110 against Haldiram’s, Bikaji and Gopal; ₹5 and ₹10 noodles against Wai Wai, Maggi and YiPPee!; ₹35–55 cookies against Britannia, Parle and Bisk Farm. Everyone pays a similar general-trade cut, but Gopal keeps 7.2% and Bikaji 10.5% EBITDA.” Only Gopal and Bikaji publish margins; PepsiCo, ITC, Haldiram’s and the regionals don’t publish snack-level figures.
