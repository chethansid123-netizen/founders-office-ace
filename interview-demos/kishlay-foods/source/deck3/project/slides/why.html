WHY IT EXISTS → HOW IT’S BUILT (60–75 s). Read left to right. “Start with physics. One: a snack packet is mostly air, so a truck fills on volume long before weight — freight per rupee of sales is brutal. Two: the whole Northeast joins India through the Siliguri Corridor, about 22 km wide. Three: monsoon humidity turns old stock soggy, so freshness is taste. Four: the shopper pays in coins, so ₹5 and ₹10 can’t rise. Today a small-town shopper gets loose chanachur or stale national packs; Kishlay bridges that with local plants, ₹5 and ₹10 packs and flavours like Jolokia and Jhalmuri. The answer is hub-and-spoke. Inputs — wheat, sugar, potato and corn, palm oil — are 70 to 75% of cost. Five units: the own North Guwahati hub at 2,000 MT with a noodles plant added in 2023, plus third-party plants at Hajipur in Bihar and in Chhattisgarh at 300 MT a month each, each inside the market it serves. Tulsi Speciality Foods makes — 512 people — and Kishlay Foods owns the brands and sells. Out through four channels: two lakh-plus general-trade outlets in 17-plus states, BigBasket, exports to Bhutan, and co-packing for Frito-Lay and Britannia. So: making near the eater beats trucking air.”
