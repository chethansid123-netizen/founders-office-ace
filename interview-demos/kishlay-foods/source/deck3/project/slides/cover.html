THE HOOK (45 s). “Kishlay is a machine that turns about 2,600 tonnes a month of potato, wheat flour, corn, palm oil, gram flour and sugar into ₹5 and ₹10 packs. Five numbers tell the story. One: it makes at three plant sites — five units — inside the markets it serves, because a truck of snacks is mostly air. Two: raw materials are 70 to 75% of cost, per CRISIL — on a price that can’t move. Three: by my estimate only about 50 to 75% of capacity is used. Four: about ₹17k of sales per outlet a year, against roughly ₹29k at Gopal. Five: 236 products on the website, 292-plus SKUs claimed, 900-plus counting every pack size — all of which has to be stocked and financed. Revenue was ₹344 Cr in FY24, through two lakh-plus outlets in 317 towns across 17-plus states. The next nine slides walk the machine left to right: why it exists, how it makes, what it sells, how it moves, who it fights, and what I’d fix.” Working: ₹344 Cr ÷ 2 lakh ≈ ₹17.2k; Gopal ₹1,468 Cr ÷ ~5 lakh ≈ ₹29k. ₹344 Cr ÷ ₹150–220/kg = 15,600–22,900 MT vs 31,200 MT capacity.
