WHERE TO PLAY (75–90 s). Four lenses, one answer. “The blue ocean canvas compares how three types of player compete on eight factors. The nationals — grey dashes — win on ad spend, national reach and premium family packs. Loose local namkeen — yellow — wins only on price and freshness. Kishlay — red — matches the cheapest on price and beats both on things nobody else combines: made locally, regional flavour, kids’ fun with toys in the pack, and range breadth. The ERRC grid turns that into action: eliminate look-alike SKUs like Onion Ringo and Onion Rin-Goo; reduce new ₹5 shapes and me-too biscuits; raise Jolokia, Jhalmuri and 40 Aana in ₹55–110 packs; create an NE-flavour range for Bhutan, Nepal and quick commerce. The BCG view — directional, because share by category isn’t public: the cash cow is the ₹5 extruded engine, about 120 products and, per the company, number one in extruded snacks in the Northeast — protect it. The star is namkeen, 39 products — invest in 200–400 g packs. Question marks are potato chips, 17 products against Lay’s, and noodles, six against Wai Wai — decide. Dogs are biscuits and pantry against Britannia and Parle — prune or co-pack. Ansoff orders the growth: first, penetration — more per outlet in the Northeast, ₹17k toward ₹23k; second, the same packs in new markets — Bihar and Bengal via Hajipur, Bhutan, Nepal, quick commerce in Guwahati; third, regional premium products; fourth, diversification — not now, with 18 categories already.”
