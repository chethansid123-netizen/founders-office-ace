Founder’s Office interview · Kishlay Foods / Tulsi Speciality Foods

The playbook: how to present “Kishlay Foods, Decoded”

What to say on each of the nine slides, the numbers to have in your head, the questions they will probably ask (with answers), and the four questions you should ask them.

Total run time: 10–12 minutes, then conversationDeck: Kishlay-Foods-Decoded.pptx / .pdf, or present from the Slides linkPrepared: 17 Nov 2026
Your one-sentence thesis. Kishlay has already won the hardest part in the Northeast — it is on two lakh shelves, made close to where it’s eaten. The next decade is about turning that availability into preference, measured by one number: sales per active outlet.

1. The story spine (memorise this, not the slides)

Every slide answers the question the previous slide raised. If you lose your place, come back to this chain:

  1. Hook. Same ₹10 packet: Kishlay keeps ~25 paise, Gopal ~52, Bikaji ~76. Why?
  2. Why Kishlay exists. Physics: a ₹5 packet is mostly air, the Northeast hangs off one narrow corridor, humidity kills crunch, MRP is a coin → make it where it’s eaten.
  3. What it built. A pack for every coin (₹5 → ₹250) and every hour; 236 products, 18 categories.
  4. Make. 2,600 MT a month from five plants; inputs are 70–75% of cost; capacity is probably only 50–75% used → demand is the limit.
  5. Move. Five hops to the kirana; follow the ₹10 down to 25 paise; ~₹17k sales per outlet a year.
  6. Sell. The retailer picks what hangs; the shopper decides in three seconds on colour, mascot, toy.
  7. Fight. Squeezed by nationals, regionals and loose namkeen; peers earn 2–3× more per packet → the gap is pull, not the factory.
  8. Brand & digital. Strong salience, weak resonance; the website leaks trust (eight concrete issues).
  9. Plan. One metric (sales per active outlet), four moves, 90 days, and what you would own.

2. Slide-by-slide talk track

The full script for each slide is also in the speaker notes of the PPTX and the Slides deck. Below: the line to land, the number to say, and how to hand over to the next slide.

01

Hook · 45 s

“Kishlay has won the shelf. Now it must win the craving.”

“Same ten-rupee packet. By my estimate from CRISIL’s public margin range, Kishlay keeps about 25 paise of it as operating profit. Gopal keeps about 52. Bikaji about 76. All three can make a good namkeen — the difference is pull.”

Say“estimate” out loud, and invite correction: “You’ll know the real number — I’d love to know how close this is.”

Handover“To see why, start from first principles: why should a Northeast snack company exist at all?”

02

Need gap · 60–75 s

Four physical facts → “make it where it’s eaten.”

Mostly air (trucks fill on volume, not weight) · one narrow gateway (Siliguri Corridor, ~22 km) · humidity kills crunch · the price is a coin. Need gap: today loose chanachur and stale national packs → bridge local plants, ₹5/₹10 packs, Jolokia/Poochka/Jhalmuri → ideal fresh, hygienic, tasty, in every lane shop, for a coin.

Handover“So what did they build to fill that gap?”

03

Portfolio · 60 s

A pack for every coin and every hour.

₹5 trial (rings with toys, fryums, ₹5 muffins and tea) → ₹10 habit (Zing, peanuts, noodles) → ₹20–55 household (Potato Feast, cookies, rusk, 200 g namkeen) → ₹110–250 occasion (400 g bhujia, Gulab Jamun ₹230, Rasgulla ₹250). 236 products on the site, 78% savoury; 292+ SKUs per the About page; 900+ once every pack size is counted.

PointBreadth wins distributors (one supplier fills 18 slots) but costs working capital. That tension comes back on slide 9.

04

Make · 60–75 s

Built to beat freight, not to win on scale.

Own North Guwahati hub (2,00,000+ sq ft, 2,000 MT; chips lines at 200–1,500 kg/hr, Fabcon extruders, balls/puffs, biscuits) + 2023 noodles plant + 3P Hajipur (300 MT/mo) + 3P Chhattisgarh (300 MT/mo). Tulsi Speciality Foods manufactures (512 people); Kishlay Foods owns the brands and markets. ISO 22000, HACCP, FSSAI; six-stage QA.

“If I divide FY24 sales by ₹150–220 a kilo, they sold 15–23 thousand tonnes against ~31 thousand of capacity — 50–75% used. If that’s right, the constraint is demand, not the factory.”

05

Move + the ₹10 waterfall · 75–90 s

Five hops, and who keeps what.

Plant → C&F → super-stockist → distributor → kirana → shopper. Sales org: VP Sales → RSM (NE) → ASMs (incl. Noodles, Tripura) → sales officers in Guwahati, Silchar, Dibrugarh, Tezpur, Nagaon, Tinsukia. ₹10 → GST 0.48 → retailer ~1.60 → trade ~0.70 → ~₹7.22 reaches Kishlay → inputs ~5.05 → overheads ~1.92 → ~₹0.25 left.

Number₹344 Cr ÷ 2 lakh outlets ≈ ₹17,200 per outlet a year ≈ six ₹10 packs a day per shop. 1% dearer inputs ≈ 5 paise a pack — a fifth of the margin.

06

Shelf + shopper · 60–75 s

Customer ≠ consumer.

The retailer (customer) decides what hangs: margin, credit, turns. The shopper (consumer) decides in ~3 seconds: hunger cue → 3–4 known brands → in stock, ₹5/₹10, fresh → grab on colour, mascot, toy → taste decides repeat. Three shoppers: the kid (buys the toy), the spice-seeker (switches weekly — keep the switching inside Kishlay’s range), the chai homemaker (ritual, loyal if trusted).

ProofGauhati High Court (Kishlay v. Ridhi Sidhi, 2019): village buyers recognise packs by overall look → pack design is the brand. Justdial 4.1★ (92 reviews); “the only taste is chilli powder” → heat isn’t flavour.

07

Competition · 60 s

Squeezed from three sides; out-earned per packet.

Nationals (PepsiCo, ITC, Britannia, Parle, Haldiram’s, Bikaji, Nestlé) · East/NE regionals (Pran, Annapurna Swadisht, Bisk Farm, Anmol, Wai Wai, Yellow Diamond, Crax) · local loose namkeen and copycats. Gopal: ₹1,468 Cr, 7.2% EBITDA, ~5 lakh outlets, ~₹29k per outlet. Bikaji: ₹2,562 Cr, 10.5% EBITDA (ex-PLI).

Land it“The gap isn’t the factory. It’s pull: brand, mix and sales per outlet.”

08

Brand + website UX · 75 s

Strong on the shelf, faint in the mind.

Keller: salience strong, performance OK, judgements unproven, resonance missing. Four master brands, 10+ sub-brands; site says Kishlay, socials say Non Stop. Website: (1) Non Stop logo mirrored in the hero, (2) all 236 products “Contact for Price” beside a price filter, (3) three “Flames”, three “Butter Cookies” with no size or MRP, (4) “Chesse Balls” online and “Supper Hot” printed on the pack, (5) “since 2003” vs “since 1995” in the Google snippet, (6) all 7 About-page milestone photos 404, (7) mobile home 4.6 MB / 183 requests with no headline or button on the first screen, (8) no way to buy.

ToneHelpful, not critical: “each of these is a one-week fix — I’d happily own it.” Fix: two front doors (Non Stop for snackers, a trade portal for distributors).

09

Plan + close · 90 s

One metric, four moves, 90 days.

North star: sales per active outlet, ₹17k → ₹23k (half the gap to Gopal) ≈ +₹120 Cr a year on today’s outlets. Diagnose (SKU cube, outlet productivity, utilisation, freight ₹/kg) → Focus (25–30 hero SKUs, prune the tail, Non Stop = consumer brand, grammage playbook) → Pull (monsoon “Non Stop + chai” with NE creators, sampling, website rebuild) → Scale (Guwahati quick-commerce pilot, copy winning beats into Bihar/Bengal, push ₹20–55).

“Kishlay has done the hardest part — it’s on the shelf. I’d like to help make it the thing people ask for.”

ThenStop. Ask your first question (section 6).

3. Delivery rules

Do

  • Open with the number, not your CV.
  • Say “my estimate” every time a number is yours; name the source when it isn’t.
  • Use their words: kirana, beat, lari, super-stockist, grammage, MRP.
  • Point at the pack photos; they made those packs.
  • Pause after slides 1, 5 and 9 — those are the ones they will interrupt on. Interruptions are good.

Don’t

  • Don’t read slides. One sentence per slide is enough; the rest is in the notes.
  • Don’t defend an estimate. Ask for the real number and update the logic out loud.
  • Don’t lead with funding, valuation or the Britannia talks (see section 5).
  • Don’t call anything “bad”. Say “leak”, “gap”, “quick win”.
  • Don’t go past 12 minutes. If short on time: slides 1, 5, 8, 9.
If they say “your number is wrong”: “Thank you — that’s exactly what I hoped to learn. What’s the right figure? … OK, then the logic changes like this: …” Recompute aloud. Showing how you update is worth more than being right.

6. The four questions to ask them

Ask one after the deck, keep the rest for the end. Each one shows you thought about their business, and each answer tells you what the job really is.

1. “If you could double one number in the next 12 months — outlets, sales per outlet, or price mix — which would it be, and what’s stopping it today?”

Tests your north-star thesis against theirs. Whatever they pick is the job.

2. “What’s the real utilisation at North Guwahati, and is the binding constraint demand, freight, or working capital?”

Shows you did the capacity maths; their answer tells you whether the role leans commercial or supply chain.

3. “With 900-plus SKUs, how do you decide what to cut — and who owns that decision today?”

Surfaces the SKU-complexity and working-capital problem, and how decisions get made between promoters and managers.

4. “What would make this Founder’s Office hire a clear success at 90 days, in your words?”

Gets the success criteria on the table. Repeat it back and tie it to slide 9.

Backups

  • “Do you want shoppers to ask for Non Stop, and Kishlay to be the company behind it — or the other way round?”
  • “Is quick commerce a Guwahati test bed for 400 g and multipacks, or a channel you’d scale?”
  • “Where does growth come from next: deeper in the Northeast, or Bihar and Bengal around Hajipur?”

7. Questions they’ll likely ask you

“Why Kishlay, and why the Founder’s Office?”

“Because the hard part is done — real plants, real distribution — and the next step is a data and brand problem, which is where I add most. A Founder’s Office sits exactly where the SKU, outlet and brand decisions meet.”

“What will you do in your first 30 days?”

Slide 9, Diagnose: ride three beats with sales officers, build the SKU cube (velocity × margin), baseline sales per active outlet by town, and get the real utilisation and freight ₹/kg by lane. Deliver one weekly MIS by day 30.

“How would you raise sales per outlet?”

Lines per call (must-stock hero SKUs), drop size (₹20–55 packs, schemes on assortment not discount), visit frequency on the best outlets, and visibility (strips, dispensers). Measure weekly by beat.

“Should we go national?”

“Not yet. Depth before breadth: the freight advantage only holds near the plants. Win Bihar and Bengal around Hajipur first, with the same beat model that works in Assam.”

“What do we do when palm oil jumps 30%?”

Grammage first (coin MRPs can’t move), then mix toward higher-MRP packs, forward-buying where cash allows, and pruning SKUs that can’t absorb it. Have the playbook ready before the spike.

“Which SKUs would you cut?”

“I wouldn’t guess without the data. I’d rank all SKUs by velocity and contribution, protect the top 25–30 and anything strategic (kids’ toy packs, regional flavours), and test cutting the bottom 20% in a few beats before going wider.”

“What’s the biggest risk to Kishlay?”

Margin squeeze: commodity inflation on fixed coin MRPs while regionals out-promote and nationals out-spend. The defence is pull (so retailers stock you without extra margin) and mix.

“Is digital worth it for a ₹5 brand?”

For the ₹5 shopper, no — the shelf is the medium. For the 400 g family pack, the urban homemaker, distributors and hiring, yes — and the current site actively loses trust. Low cost, fast fix.
