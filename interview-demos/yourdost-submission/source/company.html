2. Origin story (tell it in 20 seconds)

At IIT Guwahati, a hostel-mate of Richa Singh died by suicide over placement stress. Counsellors existed on campus, but almost nobody used them because of stigma. Richa, an IIT Guwahati graduate who later went to IIM Bangalore, teamed up with Puneet Manuja (IIM Bangalore gold medallist, who had faced placement stress himself) and Prakhar Verma (computer science, BMSCE Bangalore). Satyajeet Nandekar is the fourth co-founder. Their idea: make it as easy and stigma-free to talk to an expert as to a friend, a "dost". Hence the name and the anonymous, online-first model. (Founders' interviews: YourStory 2015, Feminism in India 2016. Leadership titles vary by source: CB Insights lists Richa as CEO, Latka lists Puneet.)

Targeting

B2B2C is the core: institutions pay and individuals use. Institutional clients make up most of revenue (company statements). The B2C funnel feeds awareness and trust. The sweet spot is young urban Indians, 18–35: students under exam and placement stress, and early-career professionals with work stress, relationships and loneliness.

Positioning

"Your emotional wellness coach": a friend (dost) who is also an expert. Anonymous, stigma-free, affordable, in your language, available in 30 seconds, 24/7, on chat, call or video. It is framed as wellness (stress, relationships, career), not illness, which lowers stigma, with clinical escalation (psychiatrists) when needed.

Points of difference: earliest mover (2014) and scale (30 lakh sessions); chat-first (58% of sessions); 20+ languages; a deep institutional book (500+ organisations); profitable and lean. Versus Amaha: Amaha moved toward clinical care and hospitals; YourDOST stays an accessible, wellness-led, B2B-heavy platform.

How to evaluate the pricing: B2C sits at mid-market therapy rates (therapists ₹1,000–3,000) and is far below executive coaching (₹20,000+). Bundles (weekly plans) raise commitment and completion. The B2B per-head model gives predictable revenue, but utilisation must be driven, or renewals suffer.

6. Marketing and distribution

  • Distribution: (1) direct institutional sales to HR, CHROs, deans and government departments; (2) partners: incubators and accelerators (Upekkha, NSRCEL), campuses; (3) direct-to-consumer through the website and app (yourdost.com), chat-first; (4) on-site and face-to-face counsellors at corporate locations.
  • Marketing: Instagram and LinkedIn content (carousels, myth-busters, Decade of Wellness data), webinars and wellbeing campaigns for clients' employees, PR and founder storytelling, SEO blog and self-assessments, events such as "The Festival of Happiness" (yourdost.net), and research reports (the founder wellbeing study) that double as B2B lead generation.
  • Growth loop: client employees and students experience it → word of mouth → B2C users → more institutions.

7. Experts: who they are, how they hire

  • Who: 1,000+ psychologists, counsellors, psychotherapists, career and life coaches, and psychiatrists for escalation (2021: 900 experts).
  • Hiring bar: minimum Masters in Psychology, 3+ years of counselling experience, English plus Hindi or regional languages; some roles are face-to-face at corporate sites, with travel. Applications go to experthiring@yourdost.com.
  • Model: a mix of full-time in-house counsellors (shifts, including nights for 24/7 cover) and empanelled freelance experts paid per session.
  • Pay (reported): about ₹26k a month (psychologist) and ₹35k (senior) on Indeed; job ad range ₹31k–₹1.35L a month. Glassdoor compensation rating 2.8/5; about 60% would recommend.
  • What staff say: praised for flexible, approachable management and as a good start for freshers; criticised for low pay, heavy workload, night shifts and attrition.

10. How to pitch yourself (EIR, Founder's Office)

Say: "YourDOST has done something rare: built a profitable, debt-free mental-health business on about $1.7M of capital, with 30 lakh sessions and 500+ institutions. But revenue has been flat at about ₹20 Cr for three years. As an EIR, my job is to find and test the next growth engines. My assignment does exactly that: Q1 opens a new segment, parents, with a data-backed, calm message. Q2 attacks the utilisation problem in B2B with content that removes the 'awkward' barriers. Q3 is a new P&L, the Founders Program: a partner-led, low-CAC channel that reaches profit by Year 3 and opens team-wellness upsells. I like to do the numbers myself, ship fast, and test before scaling, like the 90-day pilot."

Questions to ask them:

  • What share of revenue is corporate vs campus vs B2C today, and what is the renewal rate?
  • What is the biggest constraint on growth right now: sales capacity, expert supply or utilisation?
  • How are you thinking about AI, for example in intake, matching or between-session support?
  • What would success look like for this role in the first 90 days?
