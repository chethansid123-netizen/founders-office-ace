The ten posts

The assignment asked for ten short, original posts. Each one is a pair: the line people say, then the line they don't. They're listed in posting order.

    What YourDOST's own content told me

    I couldn't log in to Instagram from my workspace, so I read YourDOST's blog section by section, the website and the brief. Each finding below changed a post.

    Who they serve

    The blog is organised into Student Life, Work & Careers, Love & Relationship and Self & Growth. The business is B2B, through colleges and companies.

    Posts speak to students, employees and couples, and two speak to the buyers: HR leaders, and founders with their incubators.

    How people reach them

    In the Warrior stories, people found YourDOST through a college tie-up, a college subscription, a company benefit or a university email.

    Student and employee posts say "check your college email" or "ask HR", because many already have access and don't know it.

    How they talk

    First-person stories, Hinglish, and lines like "Please talk to someone. Don't carry it alone."

    Same warmth, but every post opens on one specific moment rather than a story summary, because a moment works as a hook.

    What's missing

    No posts on AI and jobs, festivals, founders or job hunting, though all four weigh on their audience right now.

    Four of the ten fill those gaps, timed to Diwali (8 Nov), Men's Day (19 Nov) and placement season.

    What they aren't

    The site says YourDOST is "not a medical service or suicide prevention helpline".

    The heavier posts carry Tele MANAS 14416, India's free 24×7 government helpline, next to YourDOST's own CTA.

    What they value

    YourDOST's assignment briefs ask for accuracy, nothing invented, and a short "why" for every choice.

    Every post has a 1–2 sentence why, every statistic has a source, and the illustrative details are labelled as illustrative.

    Each post, in detail

    Reels play on their own when they scroll into view. Carousels swipe, or use the arrows. Story frames move with a tap on the right or left side. The PNG and MP4 files are in the repo, ready to upload.

    Posting calendar

    One post every two days, so each gets a full day of reach before the next lands. The slots follow when each audience scrolls: students after coaching, professionals late evening, HR and founders on weekday mornings.

    How I kept it from sounding robotic

    Six rules I wrote before writing any copy, then checked every post against.

    • Start inside one moment, not a topic."I picked up the phone to tell you", not "breakups are hard".
    • One true, specific detail per post.11:47 pm. 40 seconds. 92.4%. Application #147. Detail is what makes a reader think "that's me".
    • No clinical words in the hook."Mental health" never appears in a first line. The feeling comes first; the brand shows up in the last frame or the caption.
    • The language people think in.Hinglish for students, couples and families. English for founders and HR, who work in it.
    • Ask for something small.One 🪔, your name, your application count, one question at your next 1:1. Small asks get answered.
    • Never promise what the product can't.No "AI won't take your job", no "free" unless YourDOST confirms it. YourDOST's own numbers are labelled as theirs.

    Guardrails

    • Safe messagingNo methods, no graphic detail, and no crisis words in hooks. The exam reel and the Men's Day post carry Tele MANAS 14416.
    • Comment protocolAny comment that signals risk gets a reply within the hour with 14416 and a DM, and goes to YourDOST's clinical team. Nobody is "liked" and left.
    • Check before postingConfirm with YourDOST the wording on college and company access, the founders programme link, and whether any session type can be called free.
    • Native stickersThe story frames export without the poll and slider stickers. Add the real ones in the Instagram app so they're tappable.
